# A pool proxy's Creators move only after a confirmed outage, onto a proxy that answers, and are never moved back

**Status: accepted** (2026-10-28; `service/proxy_health.py`, `service/proxies.py`, scenario `2026-10-28-proxy-failover`).

An Inflowwww pool proxy (`INFLOWW_PROXIES`, a handful of ISP proxies) can stop answering: IPRoyal refuses CONNECTs provider-side (403, curl 56) and a proxy can die outright (curl 7/97). Before this, the relay retried each call five times on the same proxy (~6 s), counted the final failures (`proxy_health`, three in a row = down) and showed `proxy_down`, but nobody moved and nothing rechecked: a Creator on a dead proxy stayed dead until an operator rebound it. We decided the relay now moves the Creators of a pool proxy only after a confirmed, sustained outage, onto pool proxies that answer a probe at that moment, and never moves them back; a custom proxy's Creators are never moved. Prod's own record set the thresholds (MEASURED, `slop/evidence/20261028T111800Z-scale-vs-load-7cc9bbd67/prod/relay_ingest_retry.log`): 229 retry lines in 55 episodes on BOTH pool proxies, 0 to 32 s long, recurring about every 5 min with the ingest refresh, and every chain ended in a success. A blip like that must move nobody: an OnlyFans session is tied to the IP it logs in from, and an IP change is a security-check and logout risk (ASSUMPTION, from the owner's experience; it is why login runs from the owner's own IP and why a Browserbase login binds our proxy only after the login). So a proxy becomes `bad` only when `DOWN_AFTER` (3) consecutive final failures under the full 5-try policy (background work, every write; a read someone waits on gives up after 2 tries in ~1 s and never counts, as three of those fit inside any blip) are followed by a failed confirm probe, which runs no sooner than `CONFIRM_GAP_S` (30 s) after the first of them and not at all if any call got through meanwhile; its Creators move only after `FAILOVER_AFTER_RECHECKS` (2) more failed rechecks, at `PROXY_RECHECK_S` (20 s) doubling. The prod timeline from the constants (and prod's measured ~0.33 s per refused try): the first full-policy failure ~7.7 s after the death (0.4 + 0.8 + 1.6 + 3.2 s of pauses, 5 tries), the confirm ~38 s (bad, surfaced), rechecks ~58 s and ~98 s, the move about 100 s after the death. A blip ends before the confirm runs (at least 7.7 + 30 s after it starts, past prod's longest, 32 s), so it marks nothing bad and moves nobody. The recheck keeps doubling up to 600 s (six probes an hour for a proxy that stays dead; each probe is one GET of Cloudflare's `/cdn-cgi/trace`, a refused CONNECT when dead and about 5 to 8 KB when alive, INFERRED from the trace body size, so the cost is bandwidth-trivial). A recheck that answers, or any call that gets through, brings the proxy back as the least-used standby; its Creators stay where they are (a second IP change is a second risk), so the pool self-heals without a fail-back storm. Every move is one registry write, so the relay's existing `on_switch` evicts the moved Creators' clients and reconnects their chat sockets together. Two more probes guard the IP change itself, through one destination check (`proxies._pick_answering`) and one mover policy: a failover probes each destination first and never moves anyone onto a proxy that does not answer (a provider-wide outage would otherwise herd everyone onto the last proxy not yet confirmed bad; a failover that found none is retried at every later failed recheck), and a pool binding (`assign_pool`: the login's attach step, Browserbase's bind) keeps a Creator on its current pool proxy until that proxy's failover is due (`failover_due`, the predicate the failover runs on) and probes its pick (at most two picks, 5 s each, as the login waits on them), so a login during an outage lands on a proxy that works instead of on the dying one and being moved later. When no pick answers, a Creator already on a pool proxy stays (never moved onto a proxy that failed its probe), and one on none is bound anyway to the least-used proxy not bad, an unprobed one first, as before probing: a finished login (a phone or Browserbase login cannot be replayed) is never thrown away over a probe blip, and that proxy's own health then decides (a dead one is confirmed and failed over like any other). When every pool proxy is bad, a Creator on none is bound to the least-used of them (no probe; surfaced as `proxy_down`): a login is never lost, and the failover moves it once a proxy answers. Only an empty pool refuses a Creator on none (409, nothing bound). The bind is a compare-and-set under the registry lock, so a failover that moved the Creator while the attach probed is kept, not followed by a second move. The operator sees it: `GET /admin/proxies` carries `health.state` (`healthy` / `suspect` / `bad` / `rechecking`), `bad_since`, `next_recheck_at`, and `pool.bad`; `GET /admin/accounts` carries `proxy_down`; the Proxies table reads "unreachable since <time> (<n> refused) — recheck at <time>" and the Accounts table badges the Creator "proxy unreachable". The cost: a Creator on a dead proxy stays dead for about 100 s before moving (prod's blips never last that long, and reads during it fail as they did before), and a recovered proxy carries nobody until the next login, so after a failover the remaining proxies run one Creator-share heavier until then (with 50 Creators on 5 proxies, 10 per proxy, a failover makes it about 12 or 13 on each of the four; ASSUMPTION that OnlyFans tolerates this share, as the pool already runs 10 per IP). A provider-wide outage moves nobody and surfaces everyone, which is the only honest answer when there is no standby. The region preference (a moved Creator keeps its country when both countries are known) is inert on a live deployment until a probe has recorded a country for each proxy: the Test button or the opt-in periodic probe (`PROXY_HEALTH_INTERVAL_S`) does that; the relay never guesses a region.

## Consequences

- `service/proxy_health.py`: states, the confirm's wait, the watcher's recheck loop with backoff, `failover_due`, `_failover` (`proxies.failover_pool`), `verify_pick` (the destination probe), `assign_pool` (injects its view into `proxies.assign_pool`). `PROXY_RECHECK_S` (default 20, floor 1) is the only knob and scales the confirm's wait with it; `--break` and the scenario compress it to 2.
- `service/proxies.py` (imports nothing of proxy_health: `bad`, `due`, `geo`, `verify` are injected): `assign_pool` and `failover_pool` share `_pick_answering`; `failover_pool` probes outside the registry lock and moves in one write; `_egress_key` counts an endpoint once whether it is held as a pool or a custom entry, verified or not.
- `service/of_client.py`: a final proxy failure (`ProxyError`, or curl 7 on a proxied client, which curl_cffi raises as `ConnectionError`) is counted in `_proxy_retry`, the one funnel every raw call takes (the x-hash warm-up of a fresh client was not counted before), and only under the full policy (`RetryPolicy.counts`).
- No fail-back, no load balancing, no custom-proxy failover, no automatic replacement of a proxy that stays dead: the operator sees `bad` and acts (replace the env entry; the sync rebinds).
- The proof is `slop/scenarios/2026-10-28-proxy-failover.py`: a 3-proxy fake pool on loopback, one dies, its Creators move within budget while 200 people read and send, a login during the outage lands healthy, no send is duplicated, the revived proxy comes back as the standby, with every proxy dead nobody moves and everyone is surfaced, and a login 1 s into that pool-wide outage (no probe answers) is still saved and bound (`C_LOGIN_ALL_DEAD`). Faults `proxy-failover-never-moves`, `proxy-recheck-never-runs` and `pool-assign-drops-unanswered-login` turn only their checks red.

## Considered Options

- **Rotate on the first failed call.** Rejected: prod's blips (0 to 32 s, both proxies, every chain succeeding) would have moved every Creator several times an hour, each move an OnlyFans IP change.
- **Fail back when the proxy recovers.** Rejected: a second IP change for the same Creators for no gain; the recovered proxy is the standby and takes the next login instead.
- **Round-robin or per-call rotation across the pool.** Rejected: an OnlyFans session must keep one IP; rotation is the failure mode this ADR prevents.
- **Mark bad from counts alone, without the confirm probe.** Rejected: three full-policy failures from concurrent calls land about 7.7 s into a blip and can still be one blip; the probe, run 30 s after the first of them (past prod's longest blip), costs a few KB and answers the question directly.
- **Count every failed call, interactive reads included.** Rejected: an interactive read gives up after 2 tries in ~1 s, so three of them arrive within a second of any blip's start and a confirm run then goes through the same blip.
- **Move onto any proxy not yet marked bad, without a destination probe.** Rejected: the all-dead run herded 13 Creators onto the one proxy that happened to be confirmed last (`slop/evidence/20261028T122444Z-2026-10-28-proxy-failover-86bc`).
- **Keep the login on the least-used pick without a probe.** Rejected: a login three seconds into an outage landed on the dying proxy and was moved 12 s later (`…-a57a`), the exact double-IP-change a fresh login must not see.
- **Buy a standby proxy per region / more proxies.** Not decided here: it needs the owner's OK and money; the design works with any pool size >= 2 and degrades to "surfaced, nobody moves" at 1.
