# A Leak watch is deleted by tombstone and a retried DELETE, and purged after 30 days

**Status: proposed** (2026-11-27, `.scratch/leak-tracker/goal.md` Phases 1 and 4; ticket 10 as amended; nothing built yet).

A Leak watch's data lives in the Removal service, but the CRM events that end it (a per-Creator delete, Creator Remove at `service/accounts_admin_api.py:708-732`, an Agency unlink at `service/auth.py:760-806`, and an agency-user delete whose cascade would drop `leak_watch` and the key) only touch local rows: Remove deletes a directory and fires no foreign-key cascade, and the service may be down when it happens. We decided the service keys everything by `watch_id`, one per (Tracker tenant, CRM account), and the CRM writes a Leak watch tombstone (watch_id, agency_user_id, account_id, tenant_id, requested_at) before it drops `leak_watch`; a Relay job retries `DELETE /v1/watches/{watch_id}` until a 2xx, sending `requested_at` so the purge clock starts at the click. Remove fans out to every Agency's watch for that account, an unlink deletes only that Agency's, and a transfer is an unlink plus a fresh enrolment. The service hides the watch at once and purges it 30 days after the request; backups roll off within 30 days of the purge. A deleted `watch_id` is remembered for the tenant's life, so a late create answers 410 and is never revived. Pausing is separate: the CRM sends one paused bit and the service keeps every row. We chose this because naming the old `watch_id` means a late retry can never hit a Creator added again, and the tombstone survives a service outage that a fire-and-forget call would not. The cost: one more table and job, and a deleted watch's data outlives the click by up to 30 days (said in the consent attestation).

ASSUMPTION: the 30-day undo and purge window is acceptable, and the consent wording is legally adequate (owner review). Overturned by either; falls on purge clocks and copy.
