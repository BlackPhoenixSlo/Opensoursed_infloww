Most creators can shoot everything on a recent phone. A dedicated camera is an upgrade, not a requirement. Here is how to decide whether you need one, what to look for, and which kind of camera fits which kind of content.

## Do you need a camera at all?

Probably not yet. A phone from the last few years shoots sharp photos and 4K video, and it is always with you. Buy a camera when you hit a specific limit:

- Your shots are noisy in the low light you actually shoot in.
- You want a softer background than portrait mode can fake.
- You shoot long videos and the phone overheats or runs out of storage.
- You want to change lenses for wide room shots or tight close-ups.

If none of these is a problem, spend the money on light first. A $40 light improves every shot; a $1,500 camera in bad light still looks like bad light.

## What to look for

- **Resolution.** High-resolution stills let you crop one photo into several. For video, 1080p is the floor and 4K is worth having.
- **Low-light performance.** Larger sensors handle dim rooms better. Check sample footage from reviewers at the ISO you would use.
- **Autofocus with face and eye tracking.** Essential if you film yourself alone.
- **A flip-out screen.** Lets you frame yourself without a second person.
- **Connectivity.** Wi-Fi or Bluetooth transfer to your phone and a remote shutter save time.
- **Battery and power.** Look for USB-C charging while shooting, or buy a spare battery.
- **Video-to-still workflow.** Shooting 4K video and pulling frames gives you photos and video from one take.

## The kinds of camera, by budget

We are not naming exact models as "best", because models change every year and prices vary by country. Instead, here are the categories and who each suits.

- **Recent flagship phone.** Best all-rounder. Easy, discreet, and the edit-and-upload path is short. Weakest in very low light and for background blur.
- **Compact vlogging camera** (1-inch sensor, flip screen, built-in mic). A real step up from a phone for talking-to-camera content and well-lit sets. Small battery; buy a spare.
- **Action camera.** Waterproof, tiny, wide angle, many mounts. Good for showers, pools, travel and point-of-view footage. Poor in low light.
- **Mirrorless body with a kit or prime lens.** Best image quality and low-light performance, interchangeable lenses, strong autofocus. Bigger, pricier, and a steeper learning curve.

A used mirrorless body that is two or three generations old is often the best value in the whole list.

## Accessories that matter more than the body

1. **A light with a diffuser.** The single biggest improvement.
2. **A sturdy tripod** and a phone or camera clamp.
3. **A remote shutter.**
4. **Fast storage.** A large, fast memory card and a backup drive.
5. **A small lavalier or on-camera mic** if you talk on camera.

## Before you buy

- Rent or borrow for a weekend if you can.
- Shoot one full set and compare it side by side with your phone.
- Keep receipts. In many places equipment used for your work may be deductible; ask a qualified accountant or check your tax authority's official guidance. Not tax advice.
