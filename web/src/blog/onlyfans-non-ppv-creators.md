PPV pages can earn more per fan; non-PPV pages are simpler to run and tend to keep subscribers longer. Neither is correct for everyone. Most established creators end up with a hybrid, and the useful question is which mix fits your audience and your time.

## How the two models work

**Non-PPV (all-inclusive).** Fans pay one monthly subscription and see everything on the feed: photos, videos, streams. Income is predictable, and what any one fan can spend is capped unless you add tips or customs.

**PPV-led.** Some or all content sits behind one-time unlocks, in posts or in messages. Many PPV-led pages use a free or low-priced subscription to attract fans, then sell individual items. A committed fan can spend far more than a subscription, but income swings more month to month.

**Hybrid.** A paid subscription with a full feed, plus a small number of PPV items or custom requests for fans who want more.

## Pros and cons at a glance

Non-PPV:

- **Pro:** easy for fans to understand. "Subscribe, see everything" is a strong pitch.
- **Pro:** steadier income and usually lower churn.
- **Pro:** less selling in the inbox, so conversations feel more relaxed.
- **Con:** the subscription price has to carry your income, so it usually needs to be higher.
- **Con:** you need enough content to keep the feed fresh every week.

PPV-led:

- **Pro:** high earning potential from your most engaged fans.
- **Pro:** you can price individual items and test what sells.
- **Pro:** a free page lowers the barrier for new fans.
- **Con:** some fans dislike paying after subscribing, and say so in reviews.
- **Con:** more work per sale: previews, captions, follow-ups.

## When non-PPV is the right choice

Non-PPV tends to work if:

- you can post consistently, several times a week,
- your audience is large enough that subscriptions alone meet your income goal,
- your niche values "no extra charges" and you can advertise that clearly, and
- you prefer chatting without a sales angle.

Non-PPV creators still have income beyond the subscription. Tips, tip goals on posts and paid custom requests all fit an all-inclusive page, because the fan chooses to pay rather than being asked to.

## When PPV is the right choice

PPV tends to work if:

- you are building an audience and want a free or cheap entry point,
- you have a smaller group of fans who buy regularly,
- you take custom requests, which are naturally priced one at a time, or
- you enjoy the conversation-and-offer rhythm of selling in DMs.

If you go PPV-led, be explicit on your page about what the subscription includes, keep previews honest, and price in tiers so fans can choose a level.

## How to test a hybrid without guessing

Change one thing, measure it, and give it time.

1. **Record a baseline** for a month: subscribers, renewal rate, and revenue split into subscription, PPV, tips and customs.
2. **Pick one change.** Raise the subscription and drop PPV, or keep the price and add two PPV items a week. Not both.
3. **Tell your fans** before the change takes effect.
4. **Run it for six to eight weeks.** Short tests mostly measure noise.
5. **Compare.** Did revenue per fan go up? Did renewals drop? Is the extra work worth it?

Creators switch models as their audience changes, and that is fine. Pages that grow fast on "no PPV" sometimes add a few offers later, and PPV-heavy pages sometimes calm down to keep fans.
