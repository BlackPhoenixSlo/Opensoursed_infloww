Short answer: YouTube does not allow nudity or sexual content, and its policies also cover links that lead to it. So YouTube cannot be your storefront. What it can be is the best long-term discovery engine there is: videos that keep getting found through search for months, built around your personality and a niche next to your work.

## Read the two policies that matter

Before you upload, read these on YouTube's own help pages:

- **Community Guidelines on nudity and sexual content.** No nudity, no sexually explicit or gratifying content, and limits on suggestive content, thumbnails included.
- **External links policy.** Links in descriptions, comments, channel pages and on-screen that lead to content YouTube does not allow are themselves violations.

YouTube enforces with warnings and strikes. Its strike policy explains the details; repeated strikes in a short period can remove a channel. Monetisation rules (if you join the Partner Program) are stricter still.

That second policy is the one most growth guides skip. Read it and decide carefully what you link. The safest route is the simplest: a consistent creator name that viewers can search for on platforms where adult promotion is allowed.

## Choose content YouTube will recommend

Pick a niche next to your work that you could talk about for a hundred videos:

- **Skills:** makeup, styling, fitness, cooking, photography, lighting, editing.
- **Life:** vlogs, routines, travel, moving, hobbies.
- **The business:** many creators make educational videos about running a creator business: branding, scheduling, money management, mental health, boundaries. These are often allowed and widely watched, as long as they stay non-explicit.
- **Commentary and Q&A:** answering questions from your audience.

Make sure thumbnails follow the guidelines too. A compliant video with a borderline thumbnail can still get flagged.

## Optimise for search

YouTube is a search engine. Each video should answer something people type.

1. **Research titles.** Start typing your topic in YouTube's search bar and note the suggestions. Those are real searches.
2. **Write honest titles** with the main keyword near the start. Clickbait that doesn't match the video loses viewers fast, and watch time is what YouTube rewards.
3. **Write a real description:** two or three sentences on what the video covers, keywords used naturally, chapters with timestamps, and your other public platforms where they comply with YouTube's links policy.
4. **Design clear thumbnails:** your face, three or four words, high contrast.
5. **Add chapters and captions.** They help viewers and search.

## Use Shorts, long videos and Lives together

- **Shorts** are for discovery. Quick, trend-aware clips that bring new viewers to the channel.
- **Long videos** are for trust and search. Ten minutes of you explaining something builds more connection than fifty Shorts.
- **Lives** are for community. Q&As, get-ready-with-me streams, planning sessions.

End each long video with an end screen pointing to another of your videos, so viewers stay with you. Pin a comment that invites questions for the next video.

## Measure the right things

YouTube Studio shows views, watch time, traffic sources and search terms. Check weekly:

- Which videos get found through search (those are your evergreen assets; make more like them).
- Which ones keep viewers past the first 30 seconds.
- Which bring new subscribers to the channel.
