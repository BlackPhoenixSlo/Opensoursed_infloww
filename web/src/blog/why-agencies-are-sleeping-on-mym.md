Should your agency test mym? It is worth a look if you have creators who are plateauing elsewhere, an audience outside the US, and a team that can run a daily routine. It is not worth it if your current platforms are under-staffed. Full disclosure: Inflowwww does not support mym either, so we are also asleep on it. Here is how to decide whether to wake up.

## Why agencies look at a second platform at all

The usual reason is saturation. When a creator's main platform is crowded, growth costs more traffic and more chat hours for the same result. A second platform can help if it offers something the first does not.

mym's case, as the platform presents it, rests on three things worth checking for yourself:

- **Internal discovery.** Feeds and search that show creators to people already on the platform, so not all growth depends on outside traffic.
- **A European audience.** Useful for creators whose appeal is not US-centric.
- **Familiar formats.** Subscriptions, paid messages and a paid catalog, which agencies already know how to run.

Check current audience figures and terms on the platform's own pages before you plan around them.

## When mym is a good fit

- **Creators with traction who have stopped growing.** A stable funnel elsewhere means you can test without risking the main income.
- **Creators with European or international appeal**, especially if they speak a language the platform's audience uses.
- **Agencies that already run a routine.** Daily posts, weekly Push messages, chat coverage and catalog updates. If that routine exists, mym is a new market with a familiar playbook.

## When to wait

- Your chat coverage on the current platforms already has gaps. A new platform makes them wider.
- The creator has no content buffer. A launch with an empty feed wastes its first traffic.
- Nobody owns the weekly numbers. Without them you cannot tell a slow start from a failed test.

## What to check about the money

Before committing a creator, read the platform's own documents on:

- Payment methods offered to fans in your target countries. Fewer failed payments means fewer lost sales.
- How chargebacks and refunds are handled, and what that means for payouts.
- Commission, payout schedule and any launch terms for agencies. Get these in writing from the platform's team.

Write the answers in one place with the date you checked. Terms change.

## A three-step rollout

1. **Talk to the platform's agency team.** Ask about launch support, promotion options and who your contact is. Take notes.
2. **Prepare inventory before launch.** Around thirty captioned posts and a small paid catalog, so momentum does not stall in week two.
3. **Run the routine after launch.** Daily public posts, a weekly segmented Push, chat coverage for new subscribers, and regular catalog updates. Review at day 30 against a target you set in advance.

If the creator beats the target, add a second one. If not, write down why and move on. A test that ends cleanly is still useful.
