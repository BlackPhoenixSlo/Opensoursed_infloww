Your banner is the wide image at the top of your profile. Its job is to repeat what your picture and bio already say, in a way a visitor takes in within a second. Design it for the phone crop first, keep text to a minimum and match the look you use elsewhere.

## Start with your brand, not the canvas

Before you open a design tool, write down three things:

- **Persona:** two or three words for how you want to come across, such as playful, polished or cosy.
- **Palette:** two or three colours you already use in your photos and on other platforms.
- **Signature:** one recurring element, like a location, an outfit style or a prop.

Collect reference images into a mood board. A private board in any design tool is enough. When a fan arrives from another platform, the banner should look familiar.

## Size and the safe zone

The banner is shown wide on desktop and much narrower on phones, so parts of it get cropped depending on the device. OnlyFans changes its layout from time to time, so check the current recommended size in its own help pages before you export.

Practical rules that hold whatever the exact numbers are:

- Work on a large, wide canvas so the image stays sharp on big screens.
- Put faces, text and anything important in the middle third. Treat the edges as decoration that may disappear.
- Export as JPG or PNG and keep the file small, a couple of megabytes at most, so the page loads quickly.
- After uploading, check the profile on a phone and a laptop. Adjust and upload again if something is cut off.

## Image quality

A blurry or pixelated banner makes the whole page look careless. Use your sharpest photos, avoid enlarging small images, and do not stack heavy filters on top of each other. If you use a collage, pick photos with similar lighting so they read as one set.

## Text: less is more

Many strong banners have no text at all. If you add some, keep it to a short line, such as your name or your posting days.

- One font, large enough to read on a phone.
- High contrast against the background.
- Nothing that will be out of date next month, like a sale price.

## Tools

You do not need paid software:

- **Canva** has free banner templates and a simple collage editor.
- **Photopea** is a free, browser-based editor with layers.
- **Figma** works well if you want precise guides for the safe zone.

Whatever you use, keep the source file. Store each creator's banner sources, fonts and colour codes in A Folder™ so the next update takes ten minutes, not an afternoon.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] Matches your profile picture, bio and other platforms.
- [ ] Important elements sit in the centre.
- [ ] Sharp at full size, small file size.
- [ ] Little or no text, readable on a phone.
- [ ] Checked on a phone and a laptop after upload.
- [ ] Refreshed when your look or theme changes, for example each season.
