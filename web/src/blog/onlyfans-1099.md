OnlyFans issues a Form 1099 to eligible US creators for each tax year, early in the following year, and makes it available through your account. Non-US creators don't get one and report under their own country's rules. The form type and reporting threshold depend on IRS rules for the year, so check [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov) and your OnlyFans tax settings for the current details. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with the IRS or an accountant.

## What a 1099 is

A 1099 is an information return. The payer sends one copy to you and one to the IRS, so both sides have a record of what you were paid outside of wages.

For a creator, that means:

- The form reports what OnlyFans paid you for the year.
- The IRS receives the same figures, so what you report should reconcile with it.
- Getting a form or not doesn't change whether the income is taxable. All self-employment income is reportable, even below the form's threshold.

## Who gets one

1. **US persons.** You gave OnlyFans a Form W-9 with your name (or business name) and taxpayer ID. The 1099 uses those details. See [the W-9, explained]({{ '/blog/onlyfans-w-9/' | url }}).
2. **Above the threshold.** The IRS sets the reporting threshold, and it has changed in recent years. Check irs.gov for the year you are filing.
3. **Non-US creators** usually file a W-8BEN instead and won't get a 1099. Report to your own tax authority.

If your W-9 details are wrong (old address, wrong name, missing business entity), fix them in your OnlyFans account before the year ends, so the form matches your return.

## When and where it arrives

- **Timing:** the IRS deadline for the payer to send you Form 1099-NEC or 1099-K is January 31 of the following year (the next business day if that falls on a weekend or holiday). Check the IRS instructions for the current year.
- **Where:** check your OnlyFans account's tax or banking settings for a downloadable copy, and watch the email address on the account.
- **If it's late or wrong:** contact OnlyFans support first, using the help centre in your account. Ask for a corrected form if a figure is wrong. If you can't get a form in time, the IRS explains on irs.gov what to do when a 1099 is missing. You can still file using your own records.

## Checking the numbers before you file

Don't copy the 1099 total straight onto your return. Check it first:

1. Download your monthly earnings statements from OnlyFans for the full year.
2. Add them up and compare the total to the 1099.
3. Note whether the figure is gross (before the platform fee) or what you were paid out. Ask your accountant how to report the fee consistently.
4. If the totals differ, find out why before you file.

## Using it on your return

For a US sole proprietor, the usual flow is:

- **Schedule C (Form 1040):** report the income from the 1099 together with any income it didn't cover, then subtract allowable business expenses to get your profit.
- **Schedule SE:** calculate self-employment tax on that profit.
- **Form 1040:** carry the totals through to your return.
- **State returns:** many states tax this income too. Check your state's tax agency.
- **Form 1040-ES:** if you expect to owe tax, the IRS expects quarterly estimated payments during the year, not one bill in April; see [how to set money aside]({{ '/blog/average-taxes-to-pay/' | url }}).

To file what the 1099 reports, use [the step-by-step filing guide]({{ '/blog/declare-onlyfans-income/' | url }}).

If you run the account through an LLC or corporation, the 1099 may be in the business's name and the forms differ. That's a question for your accountant.

### Hobby or business?

The IRS has guidance on whether an activity is a business or a hobby. Signs of a business include running it to make a profit, keeping proper records, and working to improve results. Most active creators fall on the business side, which is also what allows expense deductions. Read the IRS guidance if you're unsure.

## Keep your own records

Keep every 1099, your monthly OnlyFans statements, your bank statements and your expense receipts together, for each tax year, somewhere you control and not only inside the OnlyFans account. The IRS explains on irs.gov how long to keep records after you file. If a figure is ever questioned, these are what you show.

If an agency manages your page, it should keep the same set for each creator it works with, and hand it over on request: your tax documents are yours, not the agency's.
