Short answer: look after yourself, expect the first months to be slow, build a library before you promote, and spend more time promoting than feels natural. These are the lessons experienced creators repeat most often, in the order a new page needs them.

## Look after yourself before the account

The most common early mistake is not a content mistake. It is checking the app every few minutes, sleeping badly and burning out by month two.

- Pick fixed check-in times, for example morning, mid-afternoon and evening, and answer messages in those windows.
- Turn off push notifications outside your working hours.
- Keep one full day a week with no posting, no promo and no inbox.
- Eat, sleep and move. A page you can run for two years beats a page you sprint at for six weeks.

If you notice the work affecting your mood or relationships, take a break. The page will still be there.

## Expect the first months to be slow

Most new pages grow slowly for the first two or three months, whatever they do. That is normal, not a sign you picked the wrong niche.

Set goals you control instead of goals the market controls:

- Posts per week on your page.
- Promo posts per day on other platforms.
- Messages answered within a set time.

Review subscriber numbers once a month, not once an hour. Monthly numbers show a trend. Hourly numbers only show noise.

## Build a library before you promote

A visitor who lands on an empty page has no reason to subscribe. Before your first real promotion push, have a few dozen posts live, with variety in setting, outfit, format and length.

- Shoot in batches: one session can produce a week or more of posts.
- Change one thing per batch (background, lighting, theme) so the feed does not look repetitive.
- Keep a short backlog of unposted content for weeks when you are ill or busy.

## Promote more than you create

OnlyFans has no real discovery feed, so almost every subscriber arrives from somewhere else. Creators who have been at it a while often say they spent too much of their early time making content and too little telling people it existed.

- Use several platforms, and read each one's adult-content policy before you post. Some allow explicit promotion; many mainstream ones do not.
- Make a separate set of safe-for-work teasers for the strict platforms.
- Build an audience around something you already enjoy, like cooking, fitness or gaming, and mention your page where that platform's rules allow it.

## Price with room to discount

It is easier to start at a fair price and run limited discounts than to start low and raise the price on fans who already subscribed. Existing subscribers notice increases.

- Pick a price you would be comfortable defending for six months.
- Use time-limited promotions for growth, not permanent cuts.
- Write down why you chose the number, so you can judge it later against results.

## Safety, boundaries and a schedule you can keep

These three come up in almost every list of lessons, and they deserve plain treatment.

- **Safety:** strip location metadata from photos and videos, watermark your content, use a separate email and phone number for the work, and keep identifying details such as street views and mail out of frame.
- **Boundaries:** decide what you will and will not do before your first message arrives, write it down, and apply it to every fan the same way. Some people will push. You are allowed to block them.
- **Consistency:** a modest schedule you keep is better than an ambitious one you abandon. Three posts a week, every week, reads as professional.
