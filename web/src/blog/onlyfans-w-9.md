US creators give OnlyFans a Form W-9 so the platform can report what it pays them to the IRS. The form confirms your name, tax classification, address and taxpayer identification number. It doesn't calculate or pay any tax. Download the current form and instructions from [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9). This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with the IRS or an accountant.

## What the W-9 does

- **It identifies you to the payer.** OnlyFans uses it to issue your year-end 1099 under the right name and number.
- **It certifies your details.** You confirm, under penalty of perjury, that the information is correct.
- **It doesn't withhold tax.** As a self-employed creator, you still set aside and pay your own tax through the year.

Non-US creators don't use a W-9. They normally complete a W-8BEN (individuals) or W-8BEN-E (entities) instead, and report to their own tax authority.

## Before you fill it in

Decide how you are earning:

- **As an individual or sole proprietor:** you'll usually use your own name and Social Security number (SSN), or an employer identification number (EIN) if you have one for the business.
- **Through an LLC or corporation:** the business's legal name, classification and EIN go on the form. How a single-member LLC is entered depends on how it is taxed, so read the IRS instructions or ask an accountant.

Many sole proprietors get a free EIN from the IRS so they don't have to share their SSN with every payer. You can apply on irs.gov.

## Filling it in, part by part

Follow the instructions for the current revision of the form. In outline:

1. **Name:** your legal name as shown on your tax return, or the entity's legal name.
2. **Business name:** a trading name or disregarded entity name, if you have one. Leave it blank if not.
3. **Federal tax classification:** individual/sole proprietor, LLC (with its tax classification), C or S corporation, partnership, and so on. Tick one.
4. **Exemptions:** most creators leave these blank. Check the instructions if you think one applies.
5. **Address:** where you want the year-end tax form sent. Keep it current.
6. **Taxpayer identification number:** SSN or EIN, matching the name in line 1.
7. **Signature and date:** certifying the information is correct.

## Common mistakes

- **Name and number don't match.** The name in line 1 must match the IRS records for the SSN or EIN you enter. A mismatch can trigger backup withholding notices.
- **Wrong classification.** Ticking "individual" when you've set up a corporation, or the reverse.
- **Business name in the wrong line.** The legal name goes first; any trading name goes second.
- **Old address.** Your 1099 goes to the address on file.
- **Not updating it.** If you form an LLC, get an EIN, change your name or move, submit a new W-9.
- **Typing it from memory.** Copy your EIN from the IRS confirmation letter, not from memory.

## After you submit

- Keep a copy of what you submitted, with the date.
- Expect a 1099 early the following year if you meet the reporting rules. Download it from your account's tax section.
- Report all your income on your return, with or without a 1099.
- Set aside a share of every payout for tax, and check irs.gov for the estimated payment rules.
