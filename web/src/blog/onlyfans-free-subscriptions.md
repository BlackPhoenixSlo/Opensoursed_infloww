A free page grows faster and earns mainly through paid messages and tips. A paid page grows slower but brings predictable monthly income from fans who are already committed. Many creators end up running one of each.

## The two models side by side

| | Free page | Paid page |
|---|---|---|
| Main income | Paid messages, tips, paid posts | Subscriptions, plus messages and tips |
| Growth | Faster, lower barrier | Slower, needs stronger promotion |
| Fan intent | Mixed: many browsers | Higher: everyone already paid |
| Income stability | Varies month to month | More predictable |
| Workload | Heavy messaging | Steady feed posting |

New accounts start free by default. Switching to paid means setting a monthly price in your subscription settings, within the minimum and maximum the platform allows.

## Free pages: strengths and costs

**Strengths**

- Visitors can follow at no cost, so more of them do.
- A large follower list gives every paid message a bigger audience.
- Good for testing which content and price points sell.

**Costs**

- No base income: if you stop messaging, income drops quickly.
- More followers who never buy anything.
- Messaging takes far more time, which often means hiring help or using templates.

## Paid pages: strengths and costs

**Strengths**

- Predictable monthly income from renewals.
- Subscribers have already shown they will pay, so engagement per fan tends to be higher.
- The feed itself is the product, which suits creators who prefer posting to messaging.

**Costs**

- Every new fan has to be persuaded to pay before seeing much.
- Growth depends heavily on promotion elsewhere.
- Raising the price later can upset existing subscribers, so start at a price you can hold.

A middle path for a paid page is free trial links: a link that gives new fans a limited free period, after which the normal price applies. They work well for promotions on a single platform or for a weekend campaign.

## Running both

Plenty of creators run a free page as the top of the funnel and a paid page as the VIP tier.

- Post teasers and previews on the free page, with a pinned post explaining what the paid page offers.
- Keep the paid page clearly better: more posts, longer sets, behind-the-scenes.
- Do not post the same content on both, or the paid page loses its reason to exist.
- Plan for the extra work. Two pages means two feeds, two inboxes and two sets of statistics.

## How to decide

Ask three questions:

1. **Do you prefer messaging or posting?** Messaging suits free; posting suits paid.
2. **Do you need predictable income, or fast growth?** Predictable points to paid; growth points to free.
3. **How much time do you have?** If the answer is "not much", pick one model and do it well.

Whatever you choose, review it after three months. Compare total income, time spent and how many fans renew, then adjust.
