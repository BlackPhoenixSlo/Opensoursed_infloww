## Decide what kind of roster you are building

Agencies looking at Fanvue usually have one of two plans: move existing creators onto a second platform, or add AI-assisted personas. They need different preparation.

- **Existing creators on a new platform:** the work is traffic, posting rhythm and pricing. Treat it like any new platform launch with a 30-day test.
- **AI-assisted personas:** the work is policy first. Disclosure, consent and chatter guidance come before any content plan.
- **Both:** keep them on separate reports. Mixing the numbers hides which part is working.

Write the plan in one paragraph per creator. If you cannot explain why a creator is moving, do not move them yet.

## Put disclosure and consent in writing

Platforms that allow AI-assisted content set rules on labelling and likeness. Read the current terms on the platform's own site and keep a dated copy. Then set your own house rules, which can be stricter.

- Every AI-assisted profile is clearly labelled as such, in the way the platform requires.
- No persona is built from a real person's likeness without their written, informed consent.
- Chatters never claim a persona is a real person when it is not. Give them a written answer for "are you real?"
- One person on the team owns compliance review, and checks new profiles before launch.

These rules protect fans, creators and your agency at the same time.

## Plan staffing before you plan content

A new platform adds chats, and chats need people.

- Estimate daily conversations per creator from a similar profile you already run.
- Assign coverage hours before launch day, not after the first busy evening.
- Train chatters on the platform's own rules, which may differ from OnlyFans in what can be sold and how.

## Measure it like any other test

- Track subscribers, revenue and chat hours per creator per week.
- Compare against the same creator's results on their main platform, per subscriber.
- Keep a stop rule: if the profile misses its target at day 30, decide in advance whether you pause, change one variable, or continue.

## Keep the tooling question separate

Do not let software decide your platform strategy. Decide which platforms suit your creators first, then pick tools that cover them.

- List the platforms each creator is on, or will be on, for the next six months.
- For each tool you consider, check which of those platforms it supports today, in its own documentation.
- Where no single tool covers everything, it is fine to run one platform from its own dashboard while you test it.
