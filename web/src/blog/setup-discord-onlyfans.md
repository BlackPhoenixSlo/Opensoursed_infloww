Short answer: a Discord server gives your fans a place to talk to you and to each other, which a subscription feed can't. Set it up with clear channels, roles that match your fan tiers, age-restricted settings where needed, and moderation from day one. Then launch it to your most engaged fans first.

## Why a server is worth it

- **Community:** fans talk to each other, not just to you. That is what makes people stay.
- **Real-time contact:** text, voice and video channels for chats, Q&As and events.
- **Feedback:** you see what fans want next in their own words.
- **A VIP space:** private channels for your top subscribers make them feel recognised.

It is also work. A server needs regular attention and moderation. If you can't give it a few minutes a day, start smaller (one announcements channel and one chat) rather than letting a big server go quiet.

## Know Discord's rules first

Read Discord's Community Guidelines and Terms of Service before you build. In short, for creators:

- Users must meet Discord's minimum age, and **adult content is only allowed in channels or servers marked age-restricted**, which only adults can access.
- Adult content is not allowed in server icons, banners, invite splashes or other places visible before someone enters an age-restricted space.
- Discord prohibits certain content categories entirely regardless of settings. Read the list.

Keep the whole server safe-for-work and free to join. Paid and explicit content stays on OnlyFans, and so does every payment: never sell access to the server or deliver paid content in it.

## Build the structure

1. **Create the server** from the plus button in Discord. Name it after your creator name and upload a safe-for-work icon and banner.
2. **Turn on Community features** in server settings. This enables rules screening, a welcome screen and announcement channels.
3. **Create categories and channels:**
   - *Start here:* rules, welcome, announcements (read-only).
   - *Community:* general chat, introductions, off-topic, content suggestions.
   - *Events:* a voice channel and a stage or event channel for Q&As.
   - *Subscribers:* private, role-locked channels for verified subscribers and VIPs, for chat and announcements only. The content itself stays on OnlyFans.
4. **Write short rules:** respect, no sharing or reposting of paid content, no harassment, no personal information, no DMing the creator unless invited, how to report problems.

## Set up roles and permissions

Roles decide who sees what. A simple set:

- **Member:** everyone after accepting the rules.
- **Subscriber:** confirmed OnlyFans subscribers. Decide how you confirm (for example, a code you send through OnlyFans messages that a moderator checks).
- **VIP:** long-term or top supporters.
- **Moderator:** trusted people who can remove messages, time out and ban.
- **You:** the owner, with two-factor authentication turned on.

Check each private channel's permissions by viewing the server as each role. It is the easiest way to catch a leak.

## Add moderation and helpful bots

- Turn on **AutoMod** for spam, mention spam and a list of blocked words.
- Set a **verification level** so brand-new accounts must wait or verify before chatting.
- Use a well-known **moderation bot** for logs and warnings, and a **role bot** for reaction or button roles. Give bots only the permissions they need.
- Recruit at least one **human moderator** before you have more than a few dozen members.

## Launch and keep it alive

1. Invite your most engaged fans first, with a personal message. They set the tone.
2. Post a welcome message and a first event date.
3. Run one regular event a week: a voice Q&A, a poll on next week's content, a behind-the-scenes post.
4. Post in announcements whenever you release something on OnlyFans.
5. Check once a month when people are active, using Server Insights if your server has access to it, or simply by watching which events fill up.
