Better photos rarely come from a better camera. They come from where the light is, where the lens is, and what is in the background. Fix those three before you spend money.

## Pick the type of shot first

Decide what a set is before you start shooting. Most feeds mix three kinds:

- **Selfies:** quick, personal, close to the camera. Good for daily posts and DMs.
- **Themed sets:** a costume, a location or a story. Good for PPV and holidays.
- **Professional shoots:** a photographer, a planned set, a higher finish. Optional, and best saved for profile images and premium sets.

None is better than the others. An anonymous creator's fans may prefer casual selfies; a cosplay creator's fans expect themed sets. Look at which posts got the most replies last month and shoot more of that type.

## Gear: phone first, then three cheap upgrades

A recent phone is enough. If you want to upgrade, do it in this order:

1. **A tripod** with a phone clamp. It frees your hands, steadies the frame and lets you repeat the same angle across a set.
2. **A light.** A ring light or a small LED panel with a diffuser. Neutral white, not too close.
3. **A remote shutter or the phone's timer.** Bluetooth remotes cost very little and stop you running back and forth.

A dedicated camera comes later, if at all. Our own upgrade path is similar: $0 now, $0 later.

## Posing: a short list to rotate

Keep five poses in your pocket and rotate them so a set does not look like one photo repeated:

- **Seated:** turn your body slightly away from the lens, one leg crossed.
- **Over the shoulder:** back to the camera, look back.
- **Weight on one leg:** shift your hip, keep a hand busy (hair, hip, a prop).
- **Floor:** lie down and prop yourself on an elbow to create a line through the frame.
- **Movement:** walk toward the camera, turn, adjust an outfit. Shoot video and pull stills from it.

Stretch for five minutes first. Floor and movement poses are harder than they look.

### Angles

- **Above eye level:** softer, more intimate, good for faces.
- **Below eye level:** more powerful and dramatic.
- **Side-on:** shows shape and adds depth.

Take the same pose from all three and pick the best. That one habit triples your options at no cost.

## Lighting

- **Use window light.** Morning and late afternoon give soft light. Avoid direct midday sun, which makes hard shadows.
- **Put your main light at about 45 degrees** to your face or body, slightly above eye level.
- **Add a fill.** A second, weaker light or a white sheet opposite the main light softens shadows.
- **Switch off the ceiling light.** Overhead light puts shadows under your eyes. If it has to stay on, add a light at eye level.
- **Take test shots** before you start the real set.

## The details that save editing time

- **Clear the background.** Laundry, cables and bottles pull the eye. Two minutes of tidying saves twenty minutes of editing.
- **Lint-roll your outfit,** especially if you have pets.
- **Check the background for anything that identifies you:** mail, a window view, a framed photo, a reflection. This matters for privacy, so check it every time.
- **Shoot in the highest quality your phone offers.** You can always compress later; you cannot add detail back.
- **Edit lightly and consistently.** One preset across a set keeps it coherent.
