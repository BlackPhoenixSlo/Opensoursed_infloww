There is no correct price list for PPV. Good prices come from three inputs: how large and engaged your audience is, what the item costs you to make, and what comparable creators charge. Use a tiered ladder, test it, and let your own sales data move the prices, not a table copied from a forum.

## The three inputs

**Audience.** A creator with a large, engaged audience can charge more, because more fans compete for the same item. A new creator with few fans usually needs lower prices to get the first purchases and the first data.

**Effort and exclusivity.** A mass-sent item that took an hour to make can be priced lower, because many fans can buy it. A custom made for one fan can't be resold the same way, so price it for your time.

**Market.** Check what creators in your niche, with a similar audience size, charge for similar formats. Use it as a range, not a rule.

## Build a price ladder

Group what you sell into tiers by format and effort, from lowest to highest. A typical ladder:

1. **Single photos or short clips** sent in mass messages: your entry tier.
2. **Photo sets or longer videos:** a step up, priced for the extra effort.
3. **Premium or longer videos:** your top mass-sent tier.
4. **Custom content:** priced per item or per minute, with a minimum.
5. **Add-ons for customs:** each extra request adds to the base price.

Keep the gaps between tiers clear, so fans understand why one item costs more than another. Write a short, honest description for every item. Buyers who get what they expected buy again.

## Pricing customs

Customs take the most time, so they need the clearest rules:

- **Set a minimum** for any custom, and a per-minute rate for video.
- **Publish a menu** with starting prices, turnaround time and what you do not offer. Clear limits protect you and make the conversation simpler.
- **Take payment up front,** or a deposit for large requests.
- **Price changes as add-ons,** never as unpaid revisions.
- **Keep a record** of each request, price and delivery date.

Your boundaries are not a pricing lever. Decide what you offer first, then price it.

## Tips as part of the plan

Tips are voluntary, but you can encourage them without pressure. Thank every tipper personally, mention tips in posts now and then, and offer a small thank-you, such as an early preview, for regular tippers. OnlyFans sets limits on tip size, so check the current limits in your account.

## Test, then adjust

1. **Change one price at a time,** for one tier, for a fixed period.
2. **Compare revenue, not just purchase count.** A higher price with fewer buyers can still earn more.
3. **Look at who buys.** If the same few fans buy every tier, your ladder may be too narrow at the top.
4. **Raise prices as your audience grows.** Review the ladder every quarter.
