Broadly, OnlyFans subscribers skew male and adult-aged across a wide range, with the US the largest single market by web-traffic estimates, and creators skew female. Beyond that, platform-wide figures come from third-party estimates that disagree with each other. The numbers that actually help you are the ones about your own fans, and you already have access to them.

## What the broad picture tells you

Public estimates of OnlyFans demographics are usually based on web traffic panels and surveys, not platform data. Treat them as rough direction, not facts to plan around. The patterns most sources agree on:

- **Subscribers** are mostly men, across a wide age range from young adults to retirees.
- **Creators** are mostly women, with a growing share of men and non-binary creators.
- **Geography** is global; according to web-traffic estimates, the United States is the biggest single market, and other English-speaking countries close behind.
- **Content** is mostly adult, with real niches in fitness, cooking, music, cosplay and more.

That's enough to know the platform has room for most niches. It is not enough to tell you when to post, what to charge, or what your fans want.

## Find out who your fans are

Your own data beats any listicle. Here's where to look.

1. **OnlyFans statistics.** Your account's statistics pages show earnings, subscribers and engagement over time. Check which posts and messages earn, and when.
2. **Time of activity.** Note when replies and purchases come in. That is your audience's time zone, whatever the platform average says.
3. **Message history.** Fans tell you where they are, what they like and what they spend on. Read it as market research.
4. **Spending patterns.** Separate subscribers who never buy, occasional buyers and regulars. Each group needs a different approach.
5. **Promotion sources.** If you use tracking links on different promo channels, compare which channel brings fans who stay and spend.

## Turn it into decisions

Data is only useful when it changes what you do.

- **Posting and messaging times.** Schedule around when your fans are actually active, not around a generic "best time to post" chart.
- **Content mix.** Make more of what your regulars buy, and test new ideas on small groups before committing a month to them.
- **Pricing.** If most purchases cluster at one price point, test a step above and below it before changing anything permanently.
- **Segmenting messages.** A welcome for new subscribers, a check-in for lapsed ones and a thank-you for regulars will each do better than one message to everyone.
- **Language and tone.** If a large share of fans write in another language or from another region, adjust references and timing to match.

## Running a team? Compare per creator

Agencies running several creators should look at audience patterns one creator at a time. Two creators on the same roster can have completely different peak hours, price sensitivity and spending habits. Averaging across the roster hides exactly what you need to see. Review each creator monthly, and write down what you changed so you can tell what worked.
