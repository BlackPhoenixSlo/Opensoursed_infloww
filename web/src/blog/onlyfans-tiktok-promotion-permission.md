Short answer: TikTok does not allow nudity, sexual content, or the promotion of sexual services, and that includes directing people to adult platforms. You can still be on TikTok as a creator, but only with content that would be fine on TikTok if OnlyFans did not exist.

TikTok's Community Guidelines are the authority; read the current version yourself, because it changes.

## What TikTok's guidelines cover

TikTok's Community Guidelines have sections on sensitive and mature themes, nudity and sexual activity, and commercial and adult services. In plain terms, for creators:

- **No nudity or sexual content** in videos, Lives, photos, or profile images.
- **No promotion of adult services or platforms.** That covers captions, on-screen text, comments, bios, profile links and messages.
- **Suggestive content can be restricted** even when it is not removed, which limits who sees it.
- **Younger users are on the platform.** TikTok's minimum age is 13 in most countries, and TikTok treats adult content and anything aimed at minors with particular care. So should you.

If you are unsure whether something is allowed, TikTok's guidelines and its creator safety resources are the place to check, not a growth blog.

## What happens when rules are broken

TikTok's enforcement is described in its guidelines and its account enforcement pages:

- **Content removal**, with a notice in your inbox.
- **Restricted reach:** content that is not eligible for the For You feed, which means far fewer views.
- **Feature limits**, such as temporary restrictions on posting, commenting, Live or messaging.
- **Account bans** for severe or repeated violations.

You can usually appeal a decision from the notification or from your account status page in the app. Do that rather than reposting the same content.

Promoting adult content where minors can see it may also raise legal issues depending on where you live. If that question applies to you, ask a qualified lawyer in your country.

## What you can do within the rules

TikTok can still be part of your creator brand when you treat it as a separate, all-ages channel.

- **Post about your real interests:** fitness, cooking, fashion, comedy, gaming, travel, the creative side of your work (lighting, editing, planning).
- **Join trends** with your own spin. That is how TikTok shows you to new people.
- **Build a recognisable name and face** that people search for on other platforms.
- **Keep your username consistent** across platforms so people who want to find you elsewhere can.

What not to do:

- Don't mention OnlyFans or other adult platforms in videos, captions, comments, bios or DMs.
- Don't use coded words, emoji or "link in bio" winks to point people to adult content. TikTok's guidelines cover indirect promotion too.
- Don't put a link page in your TikTok bio that leads to adult content.
- Don't open new accounts to get around a restriction or ban. It breaks TikTok's rules.
