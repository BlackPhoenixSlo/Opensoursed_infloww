OnlyFans didn't destroy the adult industry. It moved the money. Performers who once relied on studios can now sell directly, keep most of the revenue and own their audience, and that has pulled talent and spending toward independent work. Studios still lead on budget and production scale. Here's how the two models compare, and how to decide which mix suits you.

## What changed

Before direct platforms, entering the industry usually meant going through producers, agents and casting. Studios controlled production, distribution and pay rates. Free tube sites then cut the value of studio content, putting pressure on everyone's earnings.

OnlyFans and similar platforms changed three things:

- **Distribution.** Anyone verified as an adult can publish directly to paying fans.
- **Pricing.** Creators set their own subscription, pay-per-view and custom prices. OnlyFans keeps 20% of each payment before VAT, per its terms at the time of writing; check the current figure in its Help Center.
- **Relationship.** Fans can message creators directly, which creates loyalty and repeat purchases studios can't offer.

The result is a shift of leverage. Performers now have an alternative, and studios have to compete for them.

## What studios still do better

Independence isn't automatically better. Studios still offer:

- **Production scale**: crews, sets, lighting, editing and budgets most individuals can't match.
- **Reach**: distribution networks and existing audiences.
- **Predictable pay per job**, without months of building a following first.
- **Paperwork**: contracts, releases and record-keeping handled by the producer.
- **Separation**: a shoot ends, and there are no fan messages to answer afterwards.

For a performer without an audience yet, studio work can be the faster route to income and visibility.

## Compare the two paths honestly

If you're deciding where to spend your time, work through these questions:

1. **Do you have an audience already?** Direct platforms pay off fastest when people already know you.
2. **Do you want to run a business?** Independent work means production, marketing, messaging, bookkeeping and tax.
3. **How much control matters to you?** Choosing your collaborators, content and schedule is the main draw for many people.
4. **How do you handle income swings?** Direct income varies month to month. Savings make it manageable.
5. **What are your contracts' terms?** Studio agreements may restrict your independent work. Read them, or have them reviewed.

Many performers choose both: studio scenes for reach and a steady fee, and their own page for direct income and creative control.

## If you go independent, run it properly

The responsibilities a studio handled become yours.

- **Verify every collaborator** on the platform before content is posted, and keep signed releases.
- **Only post content you own**, which usually excludes studio footage unless you have a licence.
- **Learn record-keeping obligations** for producers of explicit content where you live. In the US and some other countries, these are legal requirements. Get advice.
- **Set up bookkeeping** and tax savings from the first payout. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.
- **Protect your content** with watermarks and a plan for takedown requests.

## What it means for audiences

Fans now choose what they subscribe to rather than taking what's put in front of them. That has widened the range of bodies, identities and styles available, and rewarded performers who build real relationships with their audience. It also brings risks, such as leaks, harassment and pressure to escalate, which platforms and creators still have to manage.

## The short answer

The industry isn't being ruined. Its structure is changing: less gatekeeping, more direct income, and more responsibility for performers. The winners are those who choose their mix deliberately and run their side like a business.
