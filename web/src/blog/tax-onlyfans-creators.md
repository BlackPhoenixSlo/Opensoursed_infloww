An OnlyFans account is a business, whatever its size. That brings five practical questions: how the business is structured, who owns the content, what your contracts say, how you protect your data, and how you report income. Laws differ by country and state, so use this as a checklist to take to a qualified accountant or lawyer; none of it is legal advice.

## Business structure

Most creators start without choosing anything, which in many countries makes them a sole trader or sole proprietor by default.

- **Sole proprietor or sole trader:** simplest to run. Income goes on your personal return. You are personally responsible for the business's debts.
- **LLC or limited company:** a separate legal entity that can limit personal liability. It usually means a registration fee, annual filings and separate bank accounts. The tax effect depends on the country and how the entity elects to be taxed.
- **Corporation:** more formal, with its own tax return, and usually only worth it at higher, stable income levels.

Questions to bring to an accountant: what you expect to earn, whether you work with partners or a team, whether you want your legal name off public registers, and the total yearly cost of running each structure.

## Who owns your content

In most countries, you own the copyright in photos and videos you create from the moment they are made. Registration isn't required for ownership, though in the US it can strengthen your position in a dispute.

Practical steps:

1. **Keep originals** with their file metadata, in a backed-up folder.
2. **Know the platform's licence.** Read the OnlyFans terms on what licence you grant it to host and display your content.
3. **Use takedown notices** for leaks. Most hosts have a copyright (DMCA in the US) complaint process. Reputable takedown services can file them for you.
4. **Get written releases** from anyone else who appears in your content, stating consent, age verification, and how the content can be used and paid for.

## Contracts

Put agreements in writing before money or content changes hands:

- **Agency agreements:** the percentage, what the agency does, who controls the account and login, how either side ends the deal, and what happens to content and fan data afterwards.
- **Collaborations:** who posts what, how revenue is split, and what each person can reuse later.
- **Chatters and editors:** confidentiality, payment terms, and what they may say on your behalf.

Read every contract fully. If a clause is unclear or the term is long, have a lawyer review it before you sign. Contracts that take ownership of your content or your account deserve particular caution.

## Data security and privacy

- Use a unique password and two-factor authentication on every account.
- Keep your legal name, address and documents out of content and metadata.
- Limit who has account access. Remove access when someone leaves.
- Prefer tools that keep data where you control it, and know which third parties can see your fans' messages.

## Taxes

Creator income is usually self-employment or business income, with nothing withheld. Register with your tax authority, keep records of income and expenses, set money aside from every payout, and file on time. The official sources are the IRS at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov), HMRC at [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself) and the CRA at [canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). Sales tax, GST or VAT may also apply, depending on where you and your fans are. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.
