Your bio is the only text most visitors read before they decide whether to subscribe. A clear, personal bio turns visits into subscribers; a vague one wastes the promotion that brought them there.

## What a bio does

A visitor arrives from a link on another platform. In a few seconds they look at your picture, banner and bio, and decide. The bio is where you answer their questions before they ask:

- Who is this?
- What will I get?
- How often?
- Is it worth the price?

Every line that does not answer one of these is competing with the ones that do.

## Keep it short and personal

The field allows about 1,000 characters, but the first two or three lines do most of the work, because that is what shows before the visitor expands it.

- Lead with the most important line: what makes your page yours.
- Add one personal detail that is true and safe to share: a hobby, a running joke, a recurring series.
- Write the way you talk. If your messages are playful, the bio should be too.
- Leave out anything that identifies your real name, location or employer.

## Answer what fans want to know

Think about the visitor's decision, not your autobiography.

- **Content type**, in plain words.
- **Schedule:** "New sets every Tuesday and Friday."
- **Interaction:** whether you read messages, take requests or do customs.
- **What is where:** what is on the feed and what comes as paid messages. Being upfront prevents disappointment and refund requests.
- **Price logic,** if you run bundles or promotions: "Bundles save on three and six months."

## Use the words people search for

Discovery inside OnlyFans is limited, so most visitors come from elsewhere. Still, the words in your bio matter: they should match the words you use in your promotion, so a visitor who clicked a post about your niche sees the same niche named immediately. Use the plain terms your audience uses, not clever synonyms they would never type.

## A fill-in template

Copy this and replace the brackets:

```
[One line hook: what makes this page different]
[Who you are, in one sentence, with one personal detail]
[What you post] every [days]. [What you share in messages]
[Requests or customs: yes/no, and how to ask]
[Call to action: subscribe and say hi / check the pinned post]
```

Example: "Handmade cosplay, from sketch to final shoot. I'm a costume nerd who sews everything myself. New sets every Tuesday and Friday, process videos on Sundays. Custom requests open, message me. Subscribe and say hi; I read every first message."

## Test, do not guess

A bio is never finished.

1. Write a version and leave it for two weeks.
2. Note how many profile visitors became subscribers in that period. Your statistics show visits and new subscribers.
3. Change one line, usually the hook, and measure another two weeks.
4. Keep the better version and test the next line.

If you promote on several platforms, a separate tracking link per platform shows which audiences respond to which version.
