Facebook and OnlyFans are not competitors. Facebook is a free social network that makes money from advertising and does not allow sexual content. OnlyFans is a subscription platform where fans pay creators directly. The useful question is not which is better, but which job each one can do for you.

## Different purposes

| | Facebook | OnlyFans |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A social network for friends, communities and pages | A subscription platform for paid content |
| Who pays | Advertisers pay Facebook | Fans pay creators, and the platform takes a share |
| Content | Public or friends-only posts, groups, video | Content behind a subscription or a paid message |
| Adult content | Not allowed under its Community Standards | Allowed within its Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy |

That table explains most of what follows. Facebook is built for reach. OnlyFans is built for income from people who already know you.

## Audience and reach

**Facebook** has a huge general audience. Groups are its strongest feature for creators: focused communities around a hobby, a place or a niche. Organic reach for pages can be hard to earn, and depends on an algorithm that changes often.

**OnlyFans** audiences are smaller and paying. Fans usually arrive from outside the platform, from social media, collaborations and links you share. Once there, the relationship is direct: messages, custom requests, and content made for the people who subscribe.

For most creators, that means Facebook (where its rules allow) is a place to be found and OnlyFans is a place to earn.

## Content rules

Follow each platform's rules exactly, because the consequences of breaking them fall on you.

- **Facebook** applies Meta's Community Standards, which restrict nudity and sexual content, and also limit content that offers or solicits sexual services. Read the current Community Standards and Meta's advertising policies before you post or promote anything related to adult work.
- **OnlyFans** allows explicit content but has its own Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy listing what is prohibited, and verification requirements for everyone who appears in content. Read them in full.

If you are unsure whether a post or link is allowed on either platform, do not post it until you have checked the official policy.

## How the money works

**On Facebook**, creators typically earn indirectly: promoting their own products or services, affiliate links, sponsored content, and the platform's own creator monetisation features where eligible. Meta changes these programmes over time, so check what is available in your country.

**On OnlyFans**, creators earn directly: subscriptions, tips, paid messages and custom content. You set your own prices. The platform keeps a share of earnings, so check the current fee in OnlyFans' own terms.

## Using both well

If you decide Facebook has a place in your plan, keep it within the rules:

1. **Build a safe-for-work presence** around your personality, interests or niche, if Meta's policies allow it for your situation.
2. **Join or start groups** related to your hobbies or niche, and take part as a member, not a promoter.
3. **Keep accounts separate.** A creator identity apart from your personal profile protects your privacy.
4. **Check before linking.** Meta's policies on linking to adult platforms are strict. If in doubt, do not link.
5. **Put your effort where it pays.** Many creators find other social platforms more useful for promotion. Track where new subscribers come from, for example with a tracking link per channel, and spend your time accordingly.

## The short version

Facebook offers reach under strict rules. OnlyFans offers direct income from fans who pay. Use each for its own job, follow both rulebooks, and measure which channel actually brings you subscribers.
