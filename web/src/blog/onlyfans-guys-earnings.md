OnlyFans does not publish earnings by gender, so any claim that men earn more or less than women is an estimate. What creators commonly report is that men are a smaller share of the platform, so discovery is harder. The ones who do well pick a defined niche, market outside the platform every week, and keep the fans they win.

## A note on the numbers

OnlyFans doesn't publish creator earnings, so every figure about male creators you'll find online is an estimate. Some are reasonable, many repeat each other, and few say how they were measured. Use them for a rough sense of the spread, then measure your own account. After a few months your own numbers are worth more than any of them.

What the estimates agree on: earnings are concentrated, inactive accounts pull averages down, and a minority of male creators do very well.

## Step 1: choose a niche with a clear audience

The narrower your audience, the easier it is to find. Good niches have three things in common: an audience that already gathers somewhere, content you can make every week, and something about you that makes it yours.

Examples that work for men:

- Fitness and physique, with training logs and personal plans
- Gaming, tech or cosplay, mixing streams with subscriber extras
- Content for one specific community you already belong to
- Couples content with a consenting partner and a written agreement on money and limits
- Lifestyle: cooking, travel, style

Write your niche in one sentence. Put that sentence in your bio and your promotion profiles.

## Step 2: market every week

Without built-in discovery, marketing is most of the job.

1. **Choose two channels** where your audience already is.
2. **Post teasers** that respect each platform's rules. Read them; they differ and they change.
3. **Collaborate.** Shout-outs and joint content with creators in a neighbouring niche introduce you to an audience that already pays for creators.
4. **Give each channel its own tracking link.** You want to know which one brings paying fans, not just clicks.
5. **Cut and double.** Each month, drop the weakest channel and spend that time on the strongest.

## Step 3: turn subscribers into regulars

Winning a subscriber is expensive. Keeping one is cheap.

- **Reply quickly and personally.** Fans who feel noticed renew.
- **Remember details.** Note what each fan likes and refer back to it.
- **Offer something beyond the feed:** PPV messages, customs with a clear menu, or a members-only stream.
- **Check in with fans who stop buying,** without pressure.

A few hundred loyal fans can carry an account. Retention compounds; promotion has to be repeated.

## Step 4: handle the hard parts

Male creators can face stigma and pushback, sometimes from people close to them. It is reasonable to protect your privacy: use a separate creator name, keep personal accounts apart, and decide in advance what you share and with whom. Competition is real too. A specific niche and good conversations are the parts you control.

## Step 5: measure monthly

Track per creator: paying subscribers, renewal rate, revenue by source (subscriptions, PPV, tips, customs) and which channel each new fan came from. For a single account, OnlyFans' own Statistics page and tracking links cover all of it.
