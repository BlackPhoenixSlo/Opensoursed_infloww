On a paid page, daily PPV is usually too much, especially early on. Fans are already paying a subscription, and a lock in their inbox every day reads as spam. Start with one or two PPV messages a week, measure, and adjust from there.

## Pick the page model first

How often you send PPV depends on what kind of page you run.

- **Free page, PPV-led.** No subscription; the feed carries teasers and income comes from unlocks. Frequent PPV is expected here, because it is the product.
- **Paid page with PPV as an add-on.** Fans pay monthly for the feed, and PPV offers extras such as longer videos or special sets. This is where frequency matters most, because every offer competes with the value of the subscription.
- **Hybrids.** Some creators run a free page and a paid page side by side, or use a back-catalogue page where older PPV is available in one place. Others build informal tiers with a monthly tip that unlocks extra perks.
- **Bundles.** A discounted package of several PPV items, or a multi-month subscription with a selection of extras, can replace many small offers with one good one.

Whatever the model, the platform takes its share of each sale. Check OnlyFans' current terms for the split when you set prices.

## Starting cadence for a paid page

A sensible default for a paid page:

1. **Weeks 1 to 4:** one or two PPV messages a week, to segments rather than everyone.
2. **Fill the rest of the week with free value:** feed posts, a voice note, a casual photo, replies to every message.
3. **Never send two locks in a row** to the same fan without something free in between.
4. **Match your output.** Only schedule what you can produce at good quality. Running out of fresh PPV and resending old content does more damage than sending less.

As you learn your audience, you may move up to three or four a week, or down to one. The right number is the one that keeps renewals steady.

## Make each PPV worth opening

Frequency matters less when each offer is clearly worth it.

- **Quality over quantity.** One strong set beats four rushed ones.
- **Know your price sensitivity.** Try a few price points and notice which ones get opened. Fans paying a subscription often prefer lower PPV prices.
- **Describe the content.** Length, number of photos, theme. No guessing.
- **Publish a price menu.** A simple, branded list of what you offer and what it costs, pinned to your profile, sets expectations and saves you explaining it in every chat.
- **Personalise the send.** A short personal line before the offer ("Saw you liked the beach set, this one's from the same trip") turns a broadcast into a conversation.

## Run a four-week frequency test

Do not guess. Test.

1. **Week 0:** note your subscriber count, renewal rate, PPV open rate and revenue.
2. **Weeks 1 and 2:** send PPV twice a week.
3. **Weeks 3 and 4:** change one thing, such as moving to three a week or changing the send day.
4. **Compare:** did revenue per fan go up, and did renewals or reply rates drop?
5. **Ask fans directly.** A quick poll or a casual question ("Too many offers lately?") tells you what numbers cannot.

Keep the change that improves revenue without hurting renewals. If you cannot tell the difference, choose the lighter cadence; it costs you less work and fewer unsubscribes.
