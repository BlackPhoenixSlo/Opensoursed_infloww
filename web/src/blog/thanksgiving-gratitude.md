Thanksgiving is the one week a year when thanking fans directly reads as sincere rather than salesy. Use it. A short, specific thank-you builds more loyalty than any discount. Here are five ways to say it, and how to keep each one genuine.

## Personal thank-you messages

**Make them individual.** The fan should be able to tell the message was written for them.

- Use their name or the nickname they go by.
- Mention one specific thing: how long they have subscribed, a conversation you remember, a custom they requested.
- Keep it to two or three sentences.

Example: "Sam, you've been here since March and you still ask about my dog every week. Thank you, genuinely. Have a lovely Thanksgiving."

**Record a short video or voice note** for your longest-standing or most supportive fans. Thirty seconds is plenty.

**Time it right.** Send the night before or the morning of Thanksgiving. Avoid the middle of the day, when many fans are with family.

If you manage several creators, start from a list: fans who have subscribed longest, plus fans with notes worth mentioning. If you run Inflowwww, the fan panel in Messages Pro keeps a nickname, notes and tags per fan, shared across the team, so the specific detail is there when you write.

## Free and exclusive Thanksgiving content

- **A limited-time free post** for all subscribers, up for 48 hours.
- **Behind the scenes** of your year: the setups, the outtakes, the shoots that did not work.
- **A subscription discount** for returning fans who lapsed, sent with a thank-you rather than a sales pitch.

Do not attach a PPV price to the thank-you message itself. A thank-you with a price tag stops being a thank-you.

## A fan appreciation live

**Plan it.** Pick a time a day or two before or after the holiday, announce it a few days ahead, and decide on a rough running order.

**Encourage interaction.** Ask questions, run a quick poll, let fans pick a topic.

**Give live shout-outs** to long-time supporters, by nickname and only with their permission.

**Share news.** A teaser for December, a new series, a milestone.

**Be yourself.** This live is about connection, not production. A cup of tea and good light are enough.

## Collaborative content

**Run a content poll.** "What should my December special be?" Three options, a few days to vote, and then deliver the winner. Fans who voted feel ownership, and you get a plan for next month.

**Feature fan suggestions** that you have received during the year (with no identifying details), and say thank you for them.

## A fair Thanksgiving giveaway

Giveaways work when they are clear and honest.

- **Choose prizes** you can deliver: a custom photo set, a personal voice note or a short custom video, delivered on the platform. If you want to send anything physical, check the platform's rules first and use a service that keeps both addresses private.
- **Set clear rules:** who can enter, how (tip, like, comment), the deadline, and how the winner is picked.
- **Check the rules.** Platforms and many jurisdictions have rules on contests and prize draws, especially where entry requires payment. Read the platform's terms and, if unsure, make entry free.
- **Announce the winners publicly,** with their consent, by nickname.
- **Follow through** within the time you promised.

## Keep it sincere

- Thank fans for specific things.
- Keep sales separate from thanks.
- Reply to everyone who replies to you, even with one line.
- Write down what fans told you, so next year's message can be just as specific.
