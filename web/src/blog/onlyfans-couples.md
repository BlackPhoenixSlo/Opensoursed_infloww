Running an OnlyFans together can work well, but couples who last settle three things before they make anything: what each partner consents to, how the money and account are split, and what happens if the relationship changes. This guide takes them in that order.

## Talk it through first

Sit down together before anything is created and answer these honestly:

- **Why are we doing this?** Extra income, creativity, a full-time business? Different answers lead to different plans.
- **What is each of us comfortable with?** Write it down. Each person's list stands on its own; neither partner should feel pressured to match the other's.
- **What is off limits?** Faces, voices, locations, specific content, mentions of family or work.
- **How will we handle comments?** Decide who reads messages and how you respond to rude ones.
- **How public will this be?** Think about family, current jobs and future ones. Content online can be very hard to remove.

Consent is ongoing. Either partner can say no to a specific idea, or decide to stop altogether, at any time. Agree that out loud.

## Joint, separate or both

| Option | Good for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Joint account | A single brand, shared workload | You must agree on everything |
| Separate accounts | Different niches, individual control | Roughly double the work |
| Both | Joint content plus individual pages | The most to manage |

Whichever you choose, decide who controls each login, email address and payout method.

### Follow the platform's rules for two people

OnlyFans has its own requirements for content that features more than one person, including that everyone who appears is verified and has consented under its rules. Read the current Terms of Service and creator help pages, complete every step the platform requires for both of you before posting, and keep copies of what you submitted.

## Settle the money in writing

Even in a happy relationship, write the business side down:

- **The split.** Equal, or based on who does which work. Agree what happens to money already earned if one of you stops.
- **Where the money goes.** A dedicated account for the business keeps it separate from household money.
- **Records.** Track income and expenses, such as equipment and editing software, from the start.
- **Taxes.** Set aside part of every payout. Who reports the income depends on how you set things up and where you live. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority, on irs.gov in the US, gov.uk in the UK and canada.ca in Canada, or ask an accountant.

## Plan for a change in the relationship

Nobody likes this conversation, which is exactly why to have it now. Agree in writing:

- What happens to joint content if you separate: removed, kept, or decided case by case.
- Who keeps the account, the name and the followers.
- How remaining earnings are divided.
- That neither partner may post the other's content after a breakup without fresh consent.

This is not legal advice; have a lawyer in your jurisdiction draft or review these clauses.

## Protect your privacy

- Use business email addresses and creator names, not your legal names.
- Keep identifying details out of frame: house numbers, mail, windows with recognisable views, workplace items.
- Remove location data from photos and videos before uploading. Many phones add it automatically.
- Use strong, unique passwords and two-factor authentication on every account.
- Be careful when sharing your city, your schedule, or your relationship history with fans.

## Keep the relationship first

- Set working hours for shooting and for messages, and stop at the end of them.
- Keep some time together completely camera-free.
- Review how you both feel about it regularly, for example once a month, and adjust.
- If it starts to strain the relationship, pause. The account can wait.

If you work with a manager or an agency, give them your written boundaries list and make sure every chatter has read it.
