Fans spend more, and more often, when the conversation is clearly about them. Personalised chatting means using their name, remembering what they like, listening to what they say and marking the moments that matter to them. Here are six tactics, and how to keep them up when the inbox gets big.

## Know the fan

### 1. Use their name like a person would

Use it in the greeting and when something matters. In every message it starts to sound scripted. Use the name they gave you, or their screen name if that is all you have. If a fan corrects you or asks to be called something else, write it down immediately.

Make it a habit for every chatter: every conversation shows that you remember who this fan is, whether that is the name, a detail they shared or the last thing you talked about.

### 2. Track preferences in writing

Memory does not scale past a few dozen fans. Keep notes for each one:

- the content they open and the content they skip,
- what they have bought, at what price,
- topics they bring up outside the page: work, sport, games, pets,
- their time zone and when they usually reply,
- anything they have said they do not want.

Then use it. If a fan always opens a certain kind of set, tell them first when a new one is coming. That is a sale set up before the content exists.

If you run Inflowwww, the fan insights rail sits beside every thread in **Messages Pro** (`/messages-pro`), and **Fan reports** (`/analytics/fan-reports`) report on fan spending.

### 3. Listen actively

Personalisation is not only about content. When a fan says they had a hard week or that their team won, respond to that before anything else.

- Ask open questions: "What do you do when you're not at work?"
- Reflect back: "You mentioned the new job last week. How's it going?"
- Share something small and in persona to make it a two-way exchange.
- Use polls or a quick question ("What should I shoot next?") to invite opinions.

Fans who feel heard in the small talk are more open when the conversation turns to an offer.

## Use what you know

### 4. Turn feedback into content, and say so

Every conversation is free market research. When a fan suggests an idea or praises a particular post:

1. Thank them and note it.
2. If the idea fits your boundaries and your brand, make it.
3. Tell them: "You gave me the idea for this one."

That last step costs nothing and makes a fan feel part of the page. When you offer the content, offer it to them first.

### 5. Mark the occasions

Date-based messages are personal by definition. Worth tracking:

- **Their birthday**, if they have shared it.
- **Subscription anniversaries:** one month, six months, a year. A thank-you and a small loyalty offer work well.
- **Holidays** relevant to your audience, with themed content.
- **Your own milestones:** your birthday, the anniversary of your page, a follower count you are proud of.

Keep any time-limited offer honest. A real deadline is fine; a fake countdown that resets every week is not.

### 6. Notice when tastes change

What a fan wanted six months ago may not be what they want now. Review your notes every so often:

- Which content types are getting more engagement?
- Has a regular buyer stopped opening offers?
- Are new topics coming up in conversation?

Adjust your suggestions to match, and ask directly when unsure: "Still into the outdoor shoots, or want something different?"

## Personalising at scale without sounding canned

Templates are fine when each one is personalised before it goes out and the fan is treated honestly.

- **Write scripts with blanks** for the name and one personal detail, and never send a script with the blanks unfilled. Keep them in **Scripts** (`/growth/scripts`).
- **Segment before you mass message.** **Smart lists** (`/growth/smart-lists`) let you send to a group of fans instead of everyone, and **Smart Messages** (`/growth/smart-messages`) handles the mass and scheduled sends.
- **Follow up personally.** When a fan replies to a mass message, the next message should be written for them alone.

Personalisation is a habit, not a feature. The software just keeps the notes in one place.
