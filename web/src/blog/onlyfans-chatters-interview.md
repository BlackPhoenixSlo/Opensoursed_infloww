A good chatter interview tests four things: platform knowledge, writing quality, judgement under pressure and respect for boundaries. Charm on a video call matters less than what the candidate types when a difficult fan message lands. Build the interview around that.

## Know what the job is before you hire for it

Write a one-page role description first. A chatter typically:

- replies to fans and starts new conversations in the creator's voice,
- recommends PPV and offers when the conversation makes it natural,
- spots the fans who buy regularly and gives them extra attention,
- passes feedback to the creator about what fans are asking for,
- calms down conflicts and knows when to escalate them.

Add the practical terms: hours and time zone, number of creators, pay structure (hourly, commission or both), and the fact that the work involves adult conversations. Candidates who are not comfortable with that should be able to opt out before the interview, not during it.

## The four things to test

1. **Platform experience.** Have they worked on OnlyFans or a similar subscription platform? Ask what they did day to day and what numbers they were measured on. References are better than claims.
2. **Customer service background.** If they are new to the platform, experience in written support or sales is the next best thing. It means they can hold several conversations at once without dropping quality.
3. **Writing.** This is non-negotiable. The chatter writes in the creator's voice, so their written language has to match the creator's. Plenty of people speak a language well and write it poorly, so test in writing.
4. **Judgement and boundaries.** Will they follow the creator's limits exactly, even when a fan offers money to bend them? Will they stop and ask when unsure?

## Questions that reveal more than a CV

Ask these, and listen for specifics:

- "Walk me through a conversation where a fan who never buys finally bought. What changed?"
- "A fan says they will spend more if the creator does something on her no list. What do you reply?"
- "A regular fan is angry that you took six hours to reply. Write your answer."
- "How do you keep track of details about fans across shifts?"
- "What do you do if you realise you made a promise the creator did not approve?"
- "How many conversations can you handle at once before quality drops?"

Good answers are concrete and honest. The answer to the second question should be a polite, firm no that keeps the fan, with no hint of bargaining. Any hesitation there is the most useful signal in the interview.

## Run a live writing test

Do not rely on a sample they prepared at home.

1. Give the candidate a short persona sheet: the creator's tone, a few fixed facts, the boundaries list and a price list.
2. Send five fan messages, one at a time, in a shared document or chat: a friendly opener, a question about the creator's day, a price negotiation, a request that crosses a boundary, and a fan who has gone quiet for two weeks.
3. Give them 20 to 30 minutes.
4. Score each reply on voice, accuracy to the persona sheet, boundary handling and whether it moves the conversation forward.

Then pay for a one or two week trial on real conversations, with every message reviewed. Paying for trial work is fair, and it attracts better candidates.

## After the hire

- **Give them their own login.** Never share the creator's password. If you run Inflowwww, **Manage employees** (`/employees`) creates employee and chatter accounts, and owner-only pages are hidden from chatter sessions.
- **Share the playbook.** Persona sheet, boundaries, price list, and approved phrasings in **Scripts** (`/growth/scripts`).
- **Review weekly.** Read a sample of their conversations, not only the ones that sold. Use **Employee reports** (`/analytics/employee-reports`) for the numbers, and conversations for the quality. A chatter login puts the chatter's name on every message they send, and sales that cannot be tied to anyone wait in a **Sales needing attribution** card for the owner to assign.
- **Set a 30-day check-in** where both sides can say whether it is working.
- **Be clear about who is responsible.** A team may help with messages. OnlyFans' terms allow creators to use assistants, but the account holder stays responsible for everything sent, and fans must not be deceived about who they are talking to in a way that breaks the platform's rules or the law.

Hire for writing and judgement. Sales technique can be taught. Respect for a creator's limits mostly cannot.
