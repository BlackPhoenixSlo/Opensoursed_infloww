Your username is part of your profile URL, so it ends up in every link you share. Pick one that is short, easy to spell, available on every platform you use and unconnected to your real identity, then keep it.

## Why the username matters

- **It is your address.** Your profile link contains the username. Change it later and every old link in bios, posts and messages stops pointing to you.
- **It is your brand.** Fans type it, search for it and tell friends about it. A name that is hard to say or spell loses people.
- **It travels.** You will use the same handle on two or three promotion platforms, so it has to be free on all of them.

Your display name, the larger name above the username, can be changed more freely. Treat the username as the permanent one.

## Rules of thumb

1. **Short.** Under 15 characters if you can. Shorter is easier to remember and to type on a phone.
2. **Spellable.** If you said it out loud, could someone type it correctly the first time? Avoid unusual spellings, doubled letters and look-alike characters such as l and I.
3. **Few numbers and underscores.** They look like a fallback when the real name was taken. One is fine if it means something.
4. **Not your real name.** Keep your legal name, hometown and birth year out of it.
5. **Not someone else's name.** Avoid celebrity names, brand names and other creators' handles. Apart from the confusion, it can break platform rules on impersonation and trademarks.
6. **Fits your niche, loosely.** A hint of your persona helps, but leave room to grow if your content changes.

## Idea patterns

If nothing comes to mind, try combining two building blocks:

- **Persona word + name:** a trait or mood plus a first-name-style stage name.
- **Hobby or niche + name:** a nod to something you actually do, such as a sport, a craft or a game.
- **Alliteration:** two words starting with the same sound are easier to remember.
- **A word from another language** that matches your persona, if you can pronounce it.

Write down twenty candidates without judging them. Then cut the list using the rules above. Username generator tools can help with the first list, but check the results against the same rules.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Choosing a name that only makes sense for one season or trend.
- Picking a handle that is taken on your main promotion platform, so fans find someone else there.
- Using words that a promotion platform's filters may flag, which can limit where you can share your link.
- Changing the username after launch to chase a better one. The cost in broken links is usually higher than the gain.

## Checks before you commit

1. Search the name on OnlyFans and on every platform you plan to use. It should be free everywhere.
2. Search it on a regular search engine. Make sure it is not tied to something you do not want to be associated with.
3. Say it out loud to a friend and ask them to type it.
4. Type it on your phone keyboard with autocorrect on. If autocorrect changes it every time, fans will have the same problem.
5. Register it on every platform the same day, even the ones you will not use yet.

Then put it everywhere: bios, banners, watermarks and link pages. Consistency is what turns a handle into a brand.
