An OnlyFans manager handles the work around your content: messages, scheduling, promotion and the numbers, so you can spend more time creating. Whether you need one depends less on your follower count and more on where your week currently goes.

## What a manager does

A manager can be one person or a team at an agency. Most are paid a percentage of your earnings, a monthly fee, or a mix. The common specialities:

- **Chat.** Replying to fans, building relationships, selling paid messages.
- **Content planning.** A posting calendar, and filtering custom requests.
- **Marketing.** Promotion on social platforms and through collaborations.
- **Pricing and analytics.** What sells, at what price, to which fans.
- **Compliance.** Keeping up with the platform's rules so your account stays in good standing.

Some managers cover everything. Many are strong at one or two. Hire for the gap you actually have. A manager is a person you trust with your brand. The software they use is a separate decision, and in our case it costs $0.

## Do you need one? Run a time audit

For one normal week, write down how many hours go to each of these:

1. Creating and editing content.
2. Messaging fans.
3. Posting and scheduling.
4. Promotion on other platforms.
5. Admin: payouts, requests, planning.

If messaging and admin take more hours than creating, or if you regularly leave paying fans unanswered, a manager will likely pay for themselves. If you already keep up and enjoy the chatting, you may only need a scheduling tool and better habits.

## What to hand over first

Start small and widen the scope once trust is built:

- **First:** posting and scheduling. Low risk, easy to check.
- **Second:** chat during the hours you are offline, using scripts you approve.
- **Later:** pricing, mass messages and promotion, once you have seen a few weeks of reports.

Give them a written guide: your tone of voice, topics you never discuss, content that is never sent, and how to reach you when something needs a decision. OnlyFans' terms allow creators to use assistants, but the account holder stays responsible for everything sent, and fans must not be deceived about who they are talking to in a way that breaks the platform's rules or the law.

## What you always keep

However good the manager, some things stay with you:

- Ownership of the account, its email address and the payout details.
- The final word on what is posted and sold.
- Your boundaries list, which the manager follows without negotiation.
- A way to remove their access the same day.

A professional manager will expect this and will not ask for your bank login.

## Judge a trial month on numbers

Agree on a 30-day trial with three or four targets set upfront, for example:

- Reply time to fans during covered hours.
- Renewal rate at the end of the month.
- Paid message and tip earnings compared with the month before.
- Hours you got back.

Ask for a weekly report, not just a final one. At the end of the month, compare the numbers with the cost. If you pay a percentage, work out what that percentage is at your current earnings and at double them, so you know what success will cost too.

## Where to find one

Ask creators you trust who they work with. Creator communities and forums are a good place to ask. Talk to at least three managers before you choose, and ask each for references you can contact. The best sign is a manager who asks you a lot of questions before quoting a price.
