A costume lets you change persona for a month without confusing your audience. The trick is to pick costumes that photograph well, fit your niche and can be reused across several posts. Here are ideas by category and budget, for any gender.

## Classics that always photograph well

These need little explanation, which is the point: fans recognise them in a thumbnail.

- **Witch.** Hat, black dress, dark lipstick, a broom or LED candles.
- **Vampire.** A high collar, a cape, pale makeup, red accents.
- **Angel and devil.** Wings or horns; shoot both and let fans pick a side.
- **Cat.** Ears, a simple outfit, eyeliner whiskers. Cheap and quick.
- **Nurse, firefighter, police officer.** Use obviously costume versions with no real insignia, and follow the platform's rules on uniforms.
- **Pirate.** A shirt, a sash, boots, a hat.
- **Mummy or zombie.** Makeup-heavy, so practise once before shoot day.

Budget: $15-$40 each if you reuse basics you already own.

## Characters from games, film and anime

Character costumes do well with fans who share the fandom, and they give you an obvious hook for captions.

- Pick characters your audience already talks about. Ask in a poll.
- Shoot the iconic pose or scene, then a few casual "off-duty" frames.
- Use wigs and one key prop; the prop sells the character more than the outfit.
- Choose adult characters and style them as adults. OnlyFans' rules prohibit content that suggests a minor, so leave out school uniforms and anything childlike.
- Keep it as a costume and avoid official logos on merchandise you sell. Character fan art is common, but selling branded items can raise trademark issues.

Budget: $30-$100, more for detailed cosplay.

## DIY costumes from your own wardrobe

You probably own three costumes already.

- **Black clothes + makeup** = witch, cat, vampire or skeleton.
- **A white shirt + a tie** = any office look: boss, secretary, lawyer.
- **Gym kit + a headband** = an 80s aerobics instructor.
- **A sheet + creativity** = a ghost, a toga, a Greek statue.
- **Sports jersey** = "game day" for your sports fans.

Body paint and face paint stretch further than any bought costume. Test a patch of skin a day before for reactions.

## Couples and group costumes

If you collaborate, a paired costume doubles the content and the audience:

- Matching characters from the same story.
- Opposites: angel and devil, hero and villain.
- A group theme with other creators, all posting on the same day and tagging each other.

Agree on the posting dates and the split before you shoot. A shared calendar does the job.

## Getting a month out of one costume

1. **Teaser:** a cropped photo in early October.
2. **Vote:** "which costume should get the full set?"
3. **Main set:** the winning costume, full shoot.
4. **Behind the scenes:** the makeup process, the wig going on.
5. **Story run:** a few frames posted as stories over the week.
6. **Halloween night:** a live or a voice note in character.
7. **Recap:** a collage on November 1.

That is seven posts from one shoot.

## Shooting safely at home

- Iron and lint-roll the costume.
- Check fit in natural light, not just a mirror.
- Remove price tags and anything that identifies the store.
- Clear the background of anything that identifies your home.
- Keep candles and heat lamps well away from wigs, capes and synthetic fabric; use LED candles.
- Test body paint, glue and fake-blood products on a small patch of skin first.
