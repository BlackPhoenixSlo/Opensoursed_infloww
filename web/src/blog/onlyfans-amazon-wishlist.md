Short answer: a wishlist gives fans a way to support you beyond subscriptions and tips, and it works best when it is private by design, varied in price, and kept fresh. Set up the privacy first, then the list.

## Why a wishlist is worth the ten minutes

- **It lowers the bar to support you.** Some fans won't pay for another month but will happily send a ring light or a book.
- **It funds your content.** Lighting, backdrops, outfits and props are business costs that fans are often glad to cover.
- **It gives fans a moment.** Birthdays, holidays and milestones are natural reasons to share the list.
- **It tells fans what you actually want**, so gifts are useful rather than random.

Gifts from fans can count as business income for tax purposes; see our post on [taxes]({{ '/blog/average-taxes-to-pay/' | url }}). This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.

A gift is a gift, not a contract. Don't let a purchase create an obligation you didn't agree to, and say so in your list description if you like. Never trade content for a gift: anything a fan pays for goes through OnlyFans.

## Set up privacy before anything else

This section is the important one. Do it before you share the link.

1. **Use a separate account.** Create an Amazon account under your creator name with a dedicated email address. Never use your personal account.
2. **Don't ship to your home.** Use a P.O. box, a private mailbox service, or a mail-forwarding address. Put that address on the list, not where you live.
3. **Check the list's shipping and privacy settings.** Amazon's list settings include options about sharing your address with third-party sellers. Read each one and pick the most private choice. Amazon's help pages explain what buyers can and cannot see; read them for your country, because settings differ.
4. **Remember packaging.** Labels, packing slips and gift notes can carry names. Use your creator name on the list and the account.
5. **Never publish order confirmations or package photos** without checking them for addresses and tracking numbers.

If any of this feels uncertain, use a creator-focused wishlist service (below) that keeps your address hidden from fans by design.

## How to make the list

1. Signed in to your creator account, open Account & Lists and create a new list.
2. Choose a clear name: "[Your name]'s wishlist" works.
3. Write a short description: what the list is for and a thank-you note.
4. Set it to public or shared-by-link. Shared-by-link lets you decide who gets it.
5. Copy the link.

## What to put on it

Aim for a spread of price points so every fan can join in:

- **Small (under the price of a month's subscription):** books, candles, snacks, phone accessories, cute mugs.
- **Medium:** outfits, wigs, set decor, a tripod, a ring light.
- **Large:** a camera body, a lens, a microphone, a laptop.

Group items by theme (content gear, self-care, home and set, hobbies) so fans can browse. Ask your fans what they would like to see you use, and add the good ideas. Remove things once they arrive, and add a few new items every month so the list feels alive.

## Share it without overdoing it

- Put the link in your OnlyFans bio and on your link page.
- Post a reminder before your birthday or a holiday, then leave it alone for a while.
- Thank gifters publicly if they are happy to be named, privately if not. Ask first.
- For your top supporters, a personal message with the link works better than a broadcast.

Keep the reminder text in a note so it reads the same every time. If you run Inflowwww, save it as a reusable script in **Scripts** (`/growth/scripts`), so whoever is chatting sends the same well-written note instead of improvising one.

## Alternatives built for creators

Some creators skip Amazon and use services designed for online creators:

- **Throne:** creator wishlists with a focus on keeping your address private from fans, and options for gifting from many stores.
- **WishTender:** wishlists with privacy protection and cash-style contributions toward items.

Features and fees change, so read each service's current privacy and payout pages before you sign up, and compare them with what you need: address privacy, the stores you want, and how money reaches you.
