Five mistakes account for most disappointing OnlyFans starts: posting inconsistently, waiting for the platform to find you fans, pricing on a guess, treating privacy as an afterthought, and launching without a plan. All five are easier to avoid before launch than to fix after.

## Mistake 1: posting inconsistently

Subscribers pay for a feed. If it goes quiet, they cancel. Bursts of daily posts followed by two silent weeks are worse than a modest, steady schedule.

How to avoid it:

- **Pick a schedule you can keep on a bad week**, such as three or four posts a week.
- **Batch your production.** One or two shoot days can cover a week or more of posts.
- **Schedule posts in advance**, so the feed stays active on days you're busy or offline.
- **Tell fans what to expect**: "new sets every Tuesday and Friday" gives them a reason to stay.
- **Keep a buffer** of ready content for sick days and holidays.

## Mistake 2: not promoting elsewhere

OnlyFans has limited built-in discovery. Most subscribers arrive from somewhere else.

How to avoid it:

1. **Choose one or two promotion platforms** that suit your niche and your comfort with being visible.
2. **Read each platform's rules** on adult-adjacent content and links, and follow them.
3. **Post teasers consistently**, safe for that platform, with a clear way to find your page.
4. **Join communities in your niche** and participate, rather than only advertising.
5. **Use a tracking link per channel** so you know which ones bring fans who stay and spend.

## Mistake 3: pricing on a guess

Too high and new fans won't try you. Too low and you work hard for little, and attract fans who never buy anything else.

How to avoid it:

- **Look at creators in your niche** with a similar following, and note what they charge.
- **Decide free or paid on purpose.** Free pages grow faster and earn through messages; paid pages earn per subscriber.
- **Remember the platform fee when you set prices.** OnlyFans keeps 20% of each payment before VAT, per its terms at the time of writing; check the current figure in its Help Center.
- **Price customs by effort and time.**
- **Use promotions** for new subscribers instead of permanently lowering your base price.
- **Change one thing at a time** and give it a few weeks, so you can tell what worked.

## Mistake 4: treating privacy as an afterthought

Once your real name or location is out, you can't take it back.

How to avoid it, before your first post:

- Use a stage name and a dedicated email address.
- Keep personal social accounts private and never link them to your creator accounts.
- Turn off location tagging in your camera and check files for metadata.
- Check every frame for mail, landmarks and identifying details.
- Turn on two-factor authentication, watermarking and, if you want, geoblocking for your area. Geoblocking is a filter, not a wall: anyone using a VPN can get past it.

## Mistake 5: launching without a plan

Without a plan, every day starts with "what should I post?" and income becomes unpredictable.

A one-page plan is enough:

1. **Niche and persona**: who you are on the page, in two sentences.
2. **Content calendar**: what you'll post each week for the first month.
3. **Revenue mix**: subscriptions plus at least one of pay-per-view, customs or tips.
4. **Promotion plan**: which platforms, how often.
5. **Boundaries**: what you won't create, and your messaging hours.
6. **Money**: a separate account, records of income and expenses, and savings set aside for tax. Your tax authority's website explains self-employment obligations. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.

Review the plan after the first month and adjust based on what actually sold.

## The short version

Post steadily, promote deliberately, price with evidence, protect your identity from day one, and write a plan. Do these five and you're ahead of most new accounts.
