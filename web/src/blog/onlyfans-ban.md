OnlyFans can warn, restrict or close accounts that break its Terms of Service or Acceptable Use Policy. The reliable way to protect your account is simple: read those documents yourself, follow them in spirit as well as in letter, and get answers from OnlyFans support rather than from rumours. This post explains how.

## Go to the source

Lists of "forbidden words" and secondhand stories go out of date fast and are often wrong. The documents that actually govern your account are published by OnlyFans:

- **Terms of Service**: your contract with the platform.
- **Acceptable Use Policy**: what content and behaviour are allowed.
- **Community Guidelines and the Safety & Transparency Center**: how the rules are applied, and how to report problems.

Read them once properly, bookmark them, and re-read them whenever OnlyFans announces an update. If something is unclear, ask OnlyFans support in writing and keep the reply.

## What the rules cover

Without restating the policies, the broad areas are well known and common to most platforms:

- **Legality and age.** Anything illegal is prohibited, and anything involving minors is prohibited absolutely. Everyone who appears in content must be a verified adult.
- **Consent.** Only post people who have agreed to appear, and complete the platform's release and verification process for every collaborator.
- **Ownership.** Only post content you created or have the rights to, including music and other creators' work.
- **Honest dealing.** No misleading fans, impersonation, spam or payment arrangements outside the platform where the rules forbid them.
- **Conduct.** No harassment, threats or hate speech toward anyone.
- **Restricted content categories.** The Acceptable Use Policy lists specific content types that are not allowed. Read that section closely; it is the one creators most often get wrong.
- **Personal information.** The rules limit sharing contact details, and doing so is a safety risk anyway.

## Habits that keep you compliant

Compliance is mostly routine. These habits cover the common mistakes.

1. **Verify every collaborator first.** No shoot goes live until each person in it has completed the platform's verification and release steps.
2. **Keep a rights file.** For each piece of music, stock asset or collaboration, note where it came from and what licence covers it.
3. **Write in plain, honest language.** If a caption or message is something you would feel the need to disguise, don't send it. Rewrite the idea or drop it.
4. **Brief everyone who posts or chats for you.** Agencies and assistants should read the same policies, and you remain responsible for what goes out under your name.
5. **Review new formats before launch.** For a new content type, check the Acceptable Use Policy first, and ask support if you are unsure.
6. **Keep records.** Store releases, licences and support conversations somewhere safe. They help if a question is ever raised.

## If you receive a warning or restriction

Stay calm and work through official channels.

- **Read the notice carefully.** It usually says which content or behaviour was flagged.
- **Fix it.** Remove or correct the content in question.
- **Contact OnlyFans support** through the official help centre. Be factual and polite, and include the relevant records.
- **Use the official appeal process** if you think the decision was a mistake.
- **Change your process** so it doesn't happen again: update your checklist and brief your team.

Don't try to work around a restriction, open replacement accounts, or reuse content that was removed. That turns a fixable problem into a permanent one.

## Protect the business around the account

Even fully compliant creators should plan for disruption.

- Keep your own backup of your original content files.
- Maintain a way to reach your audience that you control, consistent with the platform's rules on promotion.
- Save a cash buffer so a support delay doesn't become a financial emergency.
