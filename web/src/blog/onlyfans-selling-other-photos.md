No. You cannot sell photos you do not own or have rights to on OnlyFans. Copyright belongs to whoever created the image, the platform's terms require you to hold the rights to everything you post, and every person shown must be verified and have consented. The good news is that there are legitimate ways to build a page without shooting everything alone.

## Who owns a photo

As a general rule, copyright in a photo belongs to the person who took it, from the moment it is taken. That applies to a professional shoot and to a selfie alike.

- **Taking someone else's image off the internet** does not give you any rights to it, even if it is publicly visible.
- **Paying for a photo** does not automatically transfer copyright. What you can do with it depends on the written agreement.
- **"Fair use" and similar exceptions** are narrow. They cover things like commentary, criticism, education and parody, and they generally require a real transformation. Reselling someone's photo as content is not that.

This is general information, not legal advice. Copyright rules vary by country; if you are unsure about a specific case, ask a lawyer.

## What the platform's rules require

OnlyFans' Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy set out the requirements. Read them yourself, but the core points are clear:

- **You must own or have the rights to everything you post.**
- **Everyone who appears in your content must be a verified adult who has consented,** under the platform's own verification process.
- **Copyright complaints are investigated,** and content found to infringe is removed.
- **Breaking the rules can lead to suspension or termination** of the account.

Posting someone else's photos breaks both the copyright rule and, if they show a person, the consent and verification rules. There is no setting or workaround that changes that.

## Why it is also a bad business idea

Even setting the rules aside, it does not work:

- **Fans subscribe for a person.** If they find out the content is not yours, trust goes immediately, and so do the renewals.
- **It harms another creator.** Behind every stolen photo is someone whose work and income you are taking.
- **It cannot grow.** A page built on borrowed images has no persona, no conversations that ring true and nothing to sell that is really yours.

## Legitimate alternatives

### Collaborate

Work with other verified creators, photographers or models.

- **Agree the terms in writing:** who appears, who owns the results, where each person can post them and how revenue is split.
- **Make sure every person on camera is verified** on the platform as its rules require, before anything is posted.
- **Credit and tag collaborators** where the platform allows. Joint content often brings both audiences.

### Hire a photographer, with the right contract

Professional photos are a great investment, as long as the agreement gives you the rights you need: commercial use, on subscription platforms, for as long as you want. Ask for this in writing before the shoot.

### Use licensed material carefully

Stock images and licensed graphics can work for non-explicit uses such as banners, backgrounds or promotional posts, if the licence permits commercial use in your context. Read the licence. Many stock licences forbid use in adult contexts or on certain platforms.

### Create original content

Original content is the only thing nobody can take away from you, legally speaking.

- **Start simple.** A phone, natural light and a tidy background are enough to begin.
- **Plan a weekly shoot** so you always have a backlog.
- **Mix formats:** photos, short clips, voice notes, behind-the-scenes posts.
- **Ask your fans** what they want to see. The answers are free content ideas.
