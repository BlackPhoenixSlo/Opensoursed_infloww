OnlyFans restricts certain words in posts, captions and messages because the content those words describe is prohibited on the platform. The useful question is not "which words do I avoid" but "what content does the platform prohibit, and does my page stay well clear of it". This post points you to the official rules and shows how to build a policy that follows them. It is not a word list, and it will not help anyone work around one.

## Where the rules actually live

The authoritative sources are OnlyFans' own documents:

- **Terms of Service,** which every creator agrees to.
- **Acceptable Use Policy,** which describes prohibited content and behaviour in detail.
- **The help centre and creator support,** for questions about how a rule applies to you.

Third-party word lists, including the ones on agency blogs, are unofficial, incomplete and go out of date. Use them, at most, as a reminder to go and read the source. If a list and the official policy disagree, the policy wins.

## Understand the categories, not just the words

Restricted terms cluster around content the platform does not allow. Broadly, the published policies prohibit content involving:

- anyone under 18, or anything that suggests a person could be,
- lack of consent, including people who are incapacitated or unaware,
- violence, serious harm and dangerous practices,
- illegal activity and illegal substances,
- certain bodily fluids and functions,
- animals,
- hate speech and slurs,
- certain offers involving money or services outside the platform.

Read the Acceptable Use Policy for the exact scope. The key point: these are rules about content and conduct. They apply whether something is written, shown, role-played or implied, and whether it appears in a post, a caption, a mass message or a private chat.

## Do not try to get around filters

Some creators and chatters try alternative spellings, symbols or code words to slip past automated checks. Do not do this, and do not let your team do it.

- The rules cover the content, not the spelling. Changing the word does not change what is being described.
- Evasion looks deliberate, and platforms treat it accordingly.
- It puts the creator's account, income and reputation at risk for something they may not have approved.

If a niche you work in relies on language near these lines, the safe move is to change the content, not the vocabulary. If you are not sure whether something is allowed, ask OnlyFans support before posting, not after.

## Build a team policy

If several people write for a page, a shared written policy keeps everyone consistent.

1. **Summarise the prohibited categories** in plain language, with a link to the official policy.
2. **List the creator's own boundaries** alongside the platform's rules. Many creators are stricter than the platform.
3. **Write approved alternatives** for everyday phrases that might cause confusion. Keep these neutral and clear.
4. **Make "I'm not sure, I'll check" an acceptable answer** for any chatter, at any time.
5. **Review a sample of messages weekly** and update the policy when the platform updates its rules.

## If something goes wrong

- **Contact OnlyFans support directly** through the official help centre. Explain what happened factually.
- **Do not open new accounts** or try to work around a restriction. Follow the platform's process.
- **Review your team policy** to find out how it happened, and fix the gap.
- **Keep records** of what was posted and when, in case support asks.

The platform's rules exist to protect creators and fans. Following them closely is part of running a professional page.
