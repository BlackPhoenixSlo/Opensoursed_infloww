Most beginners earn little in their first months, and success stories are the exception, not the median. Early income moves with three things: how many people see your promotion, whether your subscription price fits your audience, and whether you sell anything beyond the subscription. Content volume on its own does not move it much.

## Set expectations before you start

- **Plan for slow months.** Treat the first three months as building, not earning. Keep your other income if you can.
- **Build a buffer.** Save enough to cover a few lean months so a quiet week doesn't turn into panic.
- **Know your share.** OnlyFans keeps 20% of each payment before VAT, per its terms at the time of writing; check the current figure in its Help Center. Tax applies to what's left. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.
- **Ignore screenshots.** Earnings posts on social media show the best month of the most visible accounts. They are not a forecast.

## Followers are not subscribers

A big following elsewhere helps, but only the people who pay count. A worked example with round numbers:

- 2,000 followers on a social platform, 1% subscribe at $10: 20 subscribers, $200 a month gross.
- 300 followers who know you well, 10% subscribe at $10: 30 subscribers, $300 a month gross.

The smaller, closer audience wins. Early on, focus on converting people who already engage, not on raw follower counts.

## Price the subscription realistically

1. **Look at creators in your niche** with a similar audience size, and note their prices.
2. **Start in the middle of that range.** Too high and strangers won't risk it. Too low and raising it later feels like a price hike to existing fans.
3. **Change one thing at a time.** Hold a price for a few weeks before judging it.
4. **Consider a free page later.** Some creators add a free page alongside a paid one to widen the top of the funnel. Start with one page and add the second only when you can keep both active.

## Sell more than the subscription

PPV messages, tips and custom content often matter more than the subscription price, even for small accounts.

- **PPV:** send paid content in messages to fans who want more, with a clear description of what they get.
- **Tips:** thank every tipper by name. Recognition brings repeat tips.
- **Customs:** publish a short menu with starting prices, turnaround and limits. Take payment before you begin.

A small group of loyal fans who buy regularly can out-earn a much larger group who only subscribe.

## Be yourself, consistently

What makes fans choose one page over another is usually personality: your interests, humour and voice. Show it on your promotion channels too, within each platform's content rules. Then keep a schedule fans can predict. A modest plan you keep beats an ambitious one you drop in week three.

## A 90-day starter plan

- **Weeks 1 to 2:** set up the page, bio and price. Pick one or two promotion channels.
- **Weeks 3 to 6:** post on a fixed schedule, reply to every message, and introduce one PPV offer.
- **Weeks 7 to 10:** add a custom menu. Use one tracking link per promotion channel.
- **Weeks 11 to 13:** review which channel brought paying fans, which PPV sold, and what fans asked for. Do more of what worked.
