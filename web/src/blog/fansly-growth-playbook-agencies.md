How do agencies grow creators on Fansly? Make the free feed work first, post on a steady rhythm, send outside traffic to the profile, and sell in chat without breaking the conversation. Fansly has a For You feed, so organic reach is a real lever. This playbook is the version we would hand a new account manager on day one.

## Start with visibility, then conversion

On Fansly, people have to see a creator before they can subscribe. Set the profile up for reach.

- Keep a healthy share of posts visible to followers, not only to subscribers. A page that hides everything gives the feed nothing to show.
- Save the strongest content for paid messages and bundles. Visibility first, sales second.
- Write a clear bio that says what the page offers and how often it posts.
- Use relevant tags on every post. Review which tags bring followers once a month and drop the ones that do nothing.

## Set a posting rhythm the team can hold

Consistency beats bursts. A creator who posts steadily for a month usually outgrows one who posts heavily for a week and then disappears.

- Start at three to five feed clips per creator per week. Raise it only when the team can keep it up.
- Batch content weekly: one shoot, one edit session, one approver, one calendar.
- Rotate older content back in every few weeks with a new caption. New followers have not seen it.
- Do not wait for perfect. A steady post beats a polished one that ships late.

## Make clips for a swipe feed

Feed content competes with the next swipe. Keep clips short and front-load the hook.

- Test short lengths first, in the range of six to fifteen seconds, and compare completion and repeat views across lengths.
- Put the most interesting moment in the first second.
- Loopable clips tend to get watched more than once, which helps reach.
- Track per-clip results in a sheet so you learn what length and format work for each creator, not in general.

## Bring outside traffic in

Feed reach grows faster when people arrive from outside and engage. Every creator with a social presence should link to the Fansly profile.

- Post teasers with a clear hook on the creator's existing social accounts, following each platform's own rules.
- Use one tracked link per source so you know which channel brings subscribers, not just clicks.
- Point every link at the creator's profile, not a generic landing page.
- Review sources every two weeks. Double the one that converts and cut the one that does not.

## Sell in chat without stopping the chat

The best-performing chat sales feel like part of the conversation, not an interruption.

- Mid-conversation, offer a small, low-priced paid message that fits what you were just talking about. Small offers keep the thread going.
- Avoid blunt tip requests. They tend to end conversations.
- Keep a library of tested openers and follow-ups. Our name for that is Scripts (`/growth/scripts`); a shared document works too.
- Note what each fan bought and when, so the next offer is relevant and never a duplicate.

## The weekly agency checklist

1. Profiles have follower-visible posts and a clear bio.
2. Each creator has three to five clips scheduled for the week.
3. Every outside link is tracked and points at the profile.
4. Chatters have the current script library and fan notes.
5. One number per creator reviewed: follower growth, plus chat revenue for the week.
