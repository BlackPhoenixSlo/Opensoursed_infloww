An OnlyFans agency takes the business side off a creator's hands (chat, promotion, planning, reporting) in exchange for a fee or a share of earnings. Starting one is easy. Running one well takes a clear offer, a proper business setup and a team process. Here are the steps in order.

## Step 1: Decide what you sell

Pick a core service and do it well before adding more. Typical options:

- **Chat and sales.** Replying to fans and selling paid messages, often in shifts.
- **Promotion.** Growing traffic from social platforms and collaborations.
- **Content planning.** Calendars, captions, scheduling.
- **Reporting.** A clear weekly view of the numbers.

Then choose a pricing model:

| Model | How it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | A percentage of the creator's earnings | Say whether it is gross or net |
| Fixed fee | A set monthly amount | Define the scope tightly |
| Hybrid | A small fixed fee plus a lower percentage | Explain it with a worked example |

Whatever you choose, show the creator what they would pay at their current earnings and at double.

## Step 2: Set up the business properly

This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with the IRS, HMRC or CRA or a professional. It is not legal advice either; for contracts and company setup, talk to a qualified lawyer.

- **Business structure.** Options usually range from operating as a sole trader to forming a company that separates your personal assets. Your government's business registration pages explain the choices.
- **Taxes.** Register with your tax authority and find out what you must report and collect. For the US see IRS.gov, for the UK see HMRC on gov.uk, for Canada see the CRA on canada.ca.
- **Records.** Open a separate business bank account and keep every invoice and receipt from day one.
- **Contracts.** Have a lawyer draft or review your creator agreement: services, pay, term, confidentiality, content ownership and exit.

## Step 3: Know the platform rules

Your agency is responsible for following OnlyFans' Terms of Service and policies on every account you work. Read them in full, and keep up with changes. Make sure everyone who appears in content is an adult who has consented and is verified as the platform requires. Never post prohibited content. Train every team member on these rules before their first shift.

## Step 4: Write a one-page plan

You do not need a 40-page document. One page with these headings is enough to start:

1. **Who you serve.** Your niche, languages, regions.
2. **What you offer.** Services and prices.
3. **How you find creators.** Channels and a weekly outreach target.
4. **What it costs to run.** Team, tools, hosting, marketing, per month.
5. **When you break even.** How many creators at what average fee.

Review it every quarter. The numbers in point 4 are where self-hosted tools change the maths: one server instead of a fee per creator.

## Step 5: Build the brand and the team

- **Name and site.** A clear name and a simple site that says who you work with, what you do, and how you report.
- **Social presence.** Pick one or two platforms where creators talk about the business, and post useful tips more than promotion.
- **First hires.** Usually chatters first. Write a training guide, a shift handover template and a "never send" list before they start.
- **Tools.** In Inflowwww: Messages Pro for the shared inbox, Growth > Scripts for reply templates, Smart lists and Smart Messages for mass messages, Auto Post and Auto Story for scheduling, Employees > Manage employees for access, and Analytics for reports.

## Step 6: Sign your first creators

- Be visible and helpful in creator communities before you pitch anyone.
- Make a specific offer: a defined service, a 30-day trial, and the exact numbers you will report.
- Onboard carefully: signed agreement, the account session captured with the creator present, team access set, first weekly report scheduled.
- Ask happy creators for referrals once you have earned them.

Start with two or three creators and get the process right. Adding the tenth is much easier when the first three run smoothly.
