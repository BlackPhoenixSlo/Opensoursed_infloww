Creators offer free subscriptions because a free follow is an easy yes. The page grows faster, and income comes from paid messages, tips and custom requests instead of a monthly fee. It works best when the free feed is planned as a path toward paid content.

## How a free page earns

A free subscription does not mean a free business. Income comes from:

- **Paid messages (PPV):** content sent by direct message with an unlock price.
- **Tips:** on posts, messages and live streams.
- **Custom requests:** personalised content at an agreed price.
- **Paid posts:** individual feed posts with a price attached.
- **A linked paid page:** some creators use the free page to sell a separate paid one.

The more followers a free page has, the larger the audience for each paid message.

## Why creators choose it

- **A lower barrier.** With so many creators on the platform, asking a stranger to pay before they know you is a big ask. Free removes it.
- **Community.** Free followers can comment, reply and get to know you, which builds loyalty before money changes hands.
- **Testing.** A free audience shows quickly which themes, formats and price points work.
- **Satisfaction and retention.** Fans who chose to pay after seeing your work feel they got value, and they tend to stay longer.

## What to put on the free feed

Think of the free feed as a trailer, not the film.

- Previews and cropped versions of paid sets.
- Behind-the-scenes clips and daily-life posts that show personality.
- Polls asking what fans want next, which doubles as market research.
- A pinned post explaining what is available in messages or on a paid page, and how to get it.
- A regular schedule, so followers come back even before they buy.

Keep the best material for paid messages. If the free feed already has everything, nobody needs to pay.

## Does a free page hurt paid subscriptions?

It can if both pages offer the same thing. It helps when the difference is clear. Run the free page as the place people discover you, and the paid page or paid messages as the place they get the full version. Mention the paid option regularly, but not in every post.

## Managing the workload

Free pages are message-heavy, and that is where burnout starts.

- Batch your content shoots and your caption writing.
- Write reusable message templates for common situations: new follower, returning fan, request for customs.
- Answer messages in set windows, not continuously.
- Track which paid messages sell, and stop sending the ones that do not.
- Take at least one day a week fully offline.

If the inbox grows beyond what you can handle, that is the point where creators usually bring in help.

## The value of free

Used deliberately, a free page is marketing that pays for itself: it grows your audience, tests your ideas and builds a community that buys. Used without a plan, it is a lot of messaging for little income. Decide which paid offers the free page leads to before you launch it.
