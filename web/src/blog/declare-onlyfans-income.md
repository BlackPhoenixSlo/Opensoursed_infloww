OnlyFans income is taxable in almost every country, it is usually treated as self-employment income, and nothing is withheld for you. You have to report it whether or not the platform sends you a form. Thresholds and deadlines differ by country and change every year, so the steps below tell you what to find, not what the numbers are. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.

## Step 1: find your official source

Before anything else, bookmark your tax authority's page for self-employed people:

- **United States:** the IRS at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov), "Self-employed individuals tax center".
- **United Kingdom:** HMRC at [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself), "Working for yourself".
- **Canada:** the CRA at [canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html), "Self-employed business income".
- **Elsewhere:** search for your national tax authority's own site. Avoid third-party tables of thresholds; they go out of date.

From that page, write down three things for the current year: the income level at which you must file, the filing deadline, and the payment deadline.

## Step 2: work out gross and net income

- **Gross income** is everything the account earned: subscriptions, tips, PPV messages, paid posts and custom content.
- **Expenses** are costs you incurred to earn that income.
- **Net income** (profit) is gross minus allowable expenses. Most tax is calculated on this.

OnlyFans pays out after its platform fee. Ask your accountant whether to report gross earnings with the fee as an expense, or the net payout, and use the same method every year. Download monthly earnings statements from your OnlyFans account and reconcile them against bank deposits.

## Step 3: track expenses through the year

Typical categories, subject to your country's rules:

- Equipment: cameras, lighting, tripods, computers
- Software: editing tools, storage, scheduling
- Promotion: ads and paid placements
- Phone and internet, in proportion to business use
- Props and wardrobe used only for the business
- Professional fees, such as an accountant
- Travel for shoots, where it has a clear business purpose

Home office claims and meals have strict conditions in most countries. Read the official guidance before you claim them.

Keep a log with date, amount, category, a one-line reason and a link to the receipt. Ten minutes a month beats a lost weekend in spring.

## Step 4: check sales tax, GST or VAT

Some countries charge sales tax, GST or VAT on digital services. Platforms collect it themselves in some places, and creators must register in others once turnover passes a threshold.

1. Read OnlyFans' own help pages on sales tax for your country.
2. Read your tax authority's guidance on registering.
3. If it is unclear who is responsible, ask a professional before you assume it is not you.

## Step 5: choose a business structure

Most creators start as sole traders or sole proprietors: simple, with business and personal tax combined. Some later form a company (an LLC in the US, a limited company in the UK, a corporation in Canada) to separate liability or for tax planning. A company usually adds paperwork, fees and separate filings. Talk to an accountant before you incorporate; the right answer depends on your income, country and plans.

## Step 6: set money aside and file on time

- Move a fixed share of every payout into a separate tax account.
- Find out whether you must pay in advance during the year (estimated payments, payments on account or instalments).
- File on time even if you can't pay everything. Late filing and late payment are usually penalised separately.
- If you have missed past years, look up your authority's voluntary disclosure process.
