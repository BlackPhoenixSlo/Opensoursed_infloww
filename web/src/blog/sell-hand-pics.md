Yes, hand pics sell, but to a specific audience. They are a niche product: a smaller market than general content, with buyers who are often loyal and know exactly what they want. Treat it as a niche business, not a shortcut, and it can be a steady side income.

## Is there a market?

There is. Hand content buyers include:

- **Hand and nail enthusiasts** who follow the niche for its own sake.
- **Jewellery and nail-art fans** who want styled, detailed close-ups.
- **Brands and stock buyers** looking for hand models for product shots (often via separate platforms or agencies, not OnlyFans).

The audience is smaller than for general content, so expect slower growth. The upside: hand pics need no face, which makes them one of the easiest anonymous niches.

## Niches that work

Pick one or two and do them well:

- **Manicures and nail art.** Fresh sets every few weeks give you a natural content calendar.
- **Jewellery.** Rings, bracelets, watches. Pairs well with brand deals.
- **Everyday moments.** Holding a coffee, typing, turning pages, playing an instrument.
- **Textures and contrasts.** Hands on silk, in water, with flour while baking.
- **Hand-care routines.** Lotion, cuticle care, a full nail routine filmed as a video.
- **Gloves and accessories.** Leather, lace, seasonal mittens.

Write down what your best-selling set has in common after a month. Then do more of that.

## Quality: shooting hands well

**Care first.** Hands are the product.

- Moisturise daily and the night before a shoot.
- Keep nails clean and shaped; a fresh manicure photographs best.
- Remove or cover bruises and scratches with light makeup, or shoot around them.

**Camera and light.**

- A recent phone is enough. Use the main lens, not digital zoom.
- Soft, diffused light: a window with a sheer curtain, or a light with a diffuser.
- Avoid harsh overhead light, which shows every crease.

**Posing and angles.**

- Relax the fingers; tension shows.
- Slight curves and gaps between fingers look more natural than a flat hand.
- Shoot from the side and slightly above, not straight down.
- Try one hand, two hands, and hands interacting with a prop.

**Editing.** Light touch only: exposure, white balance, a little sharpening. Heavy smoothing looks fake to buyers who know the niche.

Also check every frame for identifying marks: distinctive tattoos, a wedding ring you do not want on camera, or a reflection in a ring.

## Pricing

Price by effort and exclusivity, not by an imagined market rate:

- **Low-priced sets** for regular feed posts and new subscribers.
- **Mid-priced PPV** for themed sets (a new manicure, a jewellery collection).
- **Higher-priced customs** for specific requests: a nail colour, a pose, a prop.

Write your custom menu down with what is included, and stick to it. Customs take the most time, so price them that way.

**Captions and hashtags.** Be descriptive and specific: "Fresh French tips, 15 close-ups", not "new pics". Use the niche's own vocabulary on the socials you promote on, within each platform's rules.
