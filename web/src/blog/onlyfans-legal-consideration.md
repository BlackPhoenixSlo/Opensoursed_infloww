An agency carries legal responsibilities beyond the platform: making sure everyone in content is an adult who consented, handling income and taxes correctly, putting contracts in writing, and looking after the data you hold. This is an overview to help you ask the right questions. It is not legal advice. Laws differ by country and region, so talk to a lawyer and an accountant where you operate.

## Follow the platform's rules

You are responsible for every account you work on, so make platform compliance someone's job.

- **Read OnlyFans' Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy in full**, and check them again whenever they are updated. Assign one person to track changes and brief the team.
- **Content rights.** Creators may only post content they have the rights to. Everyone who appears must be verified or covered by the platform's own process for co-creators.
- **Copyright.** Music, artwork and other material in content need permission from the rights holder.
- **Prohibited content.** The platform lists content it does not allow. Turn that list into a short checklist your team uses before anything is published.

## Age and consent records

This is the area with the most serious consequences, so keep it simple and strict.

- Confirm that every creator you sign is an adult before you sign a contract, even though the platform runs its own checks.
- Keep records showing that everyone who appears in content is an adult and has consented, stored securely, only accessible to the people who need them, and kept for as long as the law where you produce requires. In the US, 18 U.S.C. 2257 sets record-keeping rules for producers of sexually explicit content; ask a lawyer how it applies to you.
- Review records periodically for every active account.
- Never post anything where age or consent is in doubt. When unsure, do not publish.

## Taxes and income reporting

This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant. Rules depend on where your agency is, where your creators are, and how money flows between you.

- **Report income correctly**, both the agency's and, where relevant, what you pay out to creators and contractors. Official sources: IRS.gov (US), HMRC on gov.uk (UK), and the CRA on canada.ca (Canada).
- **Keep records** of every payment in and out, per creator.
- **Track expenses** such as marketing, equipment and software, and ask your accountant which are deductible.
- **Check sales tax or VAT** on your services. Digital services and cross-border clients can create obligations in more than one place.
- **International creators** add complexity. Get professional advice before you sign creators in other countries.

## Contracts with creators

Have a lawyer draft or review your template. A creator agreement usually covers:

- **Services** the agency provides, and what is outside the scope.
- **Fees**: percentage or fixed, calculated on what, and paid when.
- **Term and termination**: length, renewal, notice period and what happens on exit.
- **Content ownership**: the creator's rights in their content during and after the agreement.
- **Account access**: who holds logins, and that access ends on the last day.
- **Confidentiality**, covering everyone on your team.
- **Disputes**: how disagreements are resolved, and under which jurisdiction's law.

Walk every creator through the contract and give them time to get their own advice.

### Contracts with your team

Chatters and managers see private conversations and earnings. Their agreements should cover confidentiality, acceptable conduct with fans, and data handling. Get advice on whether each person is an employee or an independent contractor in your jurisdiction, because the obligations differ.

## Data you hold

An agency stores creators' details, fan conversations and financial data. Data protection laws, such as the GDPR in the EU and the UK GDPR, may apply to you.

- Collect only what you need, and delete what you no longer need.
- Give each team member the least access that lets them do their job, and remove access on their last day.

## Write it all down

Keep a single compliance document: who checks platform rules, where verification records are stored, your contract templates, your tax deadlines, and your data policy. Review it every quarter.
