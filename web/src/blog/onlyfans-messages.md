Direct messages are where most creators earn beyond the subscription: tips, paid messages and PPV all happen in the inbox. This guide covers how to use each messaging tool well and how to keep up when the volume grows.

## Make messages more than text

OnlyFans messages can carry photos, videos and voice clips, not just text. Mixing formats keeps the inbox from feeling like a sales channel.

- **Voice notes.** A short good-morning or thank-you message in your own voice feels personal in a way text cannot. Keep them under 30 seconds.
- **Quick casual videos.** A wave, a thank-you, a glimpse of your day. They do not need to be produced, and they do not need to be explicit.
- **GIFs and short loops.** Cut a few seconds from an existing clip into a loop to tease something upcoming or just to react.
- **Exclusive photos.** Images that are not on your other socials remind fans why the subscription is worth keeping.

Rule of thumb: if a fan's last five messages from you were all locked, send something free next.

## Build a vault of ready-to-send media

Recording something new for every reply is not sustainable. Keep a library of pre-made content organised by use:

- greetings and thank-yous,
- responses to common requests,
- teasers for upcoming sets,
- PPV items, grouped by price.

Name things so anyone on your team can find them in seconds. If you run Inflowwww, the vault is **Vault Pro** (`/growth/vault-pro`), an owner page for that library; chatters attach from it in the chat composer, limited to the folders the owner grants them. A Folder™ with a sensible naming scheme is the other half of the job.

## Use scheduled and mass messages wisely

Mass and scheduled messages let you reach many fans without writing to each one, which makes them powerful and easy to overuse.

- **Segment first.** Send different messages to new subscribers, regular buyers and fans who have gone quiet. A single blast to everyone is the least effective version.
- **Schedule for your audience's time zone,** not yours. Check when your fans are most active and send then.
- **Limit the frequency.** A few well-made mass messages a week beat a daily stream that trains fans to ignore you.
- **Personalise the opener.** Even a mass message can use the fan's name or reference a recent post.

## Price PPV messages with a plan

PPV messages are content locked behind a one-time price. Some guidelines:

- **Tier your prices.** Have a low entry price for teasers or short clips, a middle price for full sets, and a premium price for long or custom content.
- **Describe what is inside.** State the length or the number of photos. Fans who know what they are buying buy again.
- **Match the preview to the content.** Overselling wins one sale and loses the fan.
- **Reward your best buyers** with early access or a loyalty price.
- **Review regularly.** Look at which price points get opened and adjust. The **Message dashboard** (`/analytics/message-dashboard`) keeps a log of PPVs, tips and messages so you can see what sold.

## Reply promptly, and never miss a fan

Fast, friendly replies keep fans subscribed. With a busy inbox, that takes a system.

1. **Set fixed inbox times** every day, and stick to them.
2. **Work the oldest unanswered chats first,** then the regulars.
3. **Use scripts for common situations** such as welcome, thank-you and follow-up, and always add a personal detail before sending. Store them in **Scripts** (`/growth/scripts`).
4. **Personalise every reply.** Use the fan's name or screen name, and refer to something they told you.
5. **Keep notes.** In **Messages Pro** (`/messages-pro`), the chat list, thread and fan insights rail sit side by side, so the context is there when you reply.

If a team helps with messages, the same system applies to everyone and the creator should be able to read any conversation at any time. OnlyFans' terms allow creators to use assistants, but the account holder stays responsible for everything sent, and fans must not be deceived about who they are talking to in a way that breaks the platform's rules or the law.

## A simple weekly messaging routine

- **Daily:** clear unanswered chats, send one personal touch (voice note, photo or check-in) to a handful of fans.
- **Twice a week:** one segmented mass message.
- **Weekly:** review which PPVs sold, refresh the vault, update scripts that sound stale.

That is enough to stay on top of the inbox without living in it.
