Short answer: open Account Status in Instagram's settings. If anything there is not green, Instagram tells you which rule it thinks you broke and often which post. Fix that, request a review where Instagram offers one, and keep future posts inside the guidelines.

Instagram's Community Guidelines and Help Center are the authority; check them when anything here differs from what you see.

## Check Account Status first

"Shadowban" is the word people use for reduced reach without a clear notice. Instagram's own tool for this is Account Status.

1. Open your profile, tap the menu, and go to Settings.
2. Find **Account Status** (it may sit under an account or help section depending on your app version).
3. Look at each category. Instagram shows whether your account and content can be recommended to non-followers, and whether any content has been removed.

If everything is fine, the drop is probably ordinary: a weaker post, a change in timing, or a shift in what your audience is watching. Compare your last two weeks of Insights with the previous two weeks before you change anything drastic.

If something is flagged, tap into it. Instagram usually lists the content or reason involved.

## Find the cause

Restrictions for creators usually come from one of three places.

**Posts and Reels.** Content Instagram considers sexually suggestive, nudity, or spam-like behaviour. Look first at the post Account Status names, then at anything recent that was close to the line.

**Profile and bio.** The profile photo, name, bio text and highlights are reviewed too. Suggestive wording, suggestive emoji combinations, or a picture that would not pass as a feed post can all count.

**Links.** Links that lead, directly or through a redirect, to adult sites are a common trigger. That includes your bio link, Story links and links in captions or comments.

If Instagram has not given a reason yet, wait a day and check again. Reviews are not always instant.

## Fix it, one change at a time

1. **Remove or edit the flagged content.** If you believe it was flagged by mistake, request a review first (next section), because deleting it removes the evidence.
2. **Clean up the profile.** Rewrite the bio in plain, non-suggestive language, replace a borderline profile photo, and review highlight covers.
3. **Review your link.** Make sure your bio link points to a page that complies with Instagram's rules. Read Instagram's guidance on links rather than looking for ways around it.
4. **Change one thing per day** and recheck Account Status, so you learn what mattered.
5. **Pause aggressive tactics** while you recover: mass following, repetitive comments, or the same caption on every post can look like spam.

## Request a review

Instagram lets you disagree with many decisions.

- From Account Status, open the flagged item and look for an option to request a review or disagree with the decision.
- You can also reach support requests and violations from the Help section of Settings.
- Keep it factual. Explain briefly why the content complies with the guidelines.

Decisions can take time. Keep posting compliant content while you wait; it does not hurt your review.

## Protect your audience going forward

- **Keep a copy of everything.** Back up your photos and videos off-platform so a removed post never means lost work.
- **Own a direct line to your fans.** An email list or a link page you control means your audience can find you whatever happens on any one platform.
- **Write down a content standard** for the account and check posts against it before publishing. Agencies should keep one standard per creator and train every person who posts.
- **Don't open new accounts to get around a restriction or a disabled account.** Instagram's terms prohibit it, and it puts every related account at risk. If an account is disabled, use Instagram's official appeal route.
