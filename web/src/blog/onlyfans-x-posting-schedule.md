Short answer: the right schedule is the one anchored to when your own followers are online, that mixes teasers, personality posts and promotion, and that you can keep every day. Generic "best time to post" charts are a starting point. Your own numbers are the answer.

## Find when your audience is actually online

**Use X's analytics.** Depending on your account type, X shows impressions and engagement per post, and sometimes audience activity. Note the hours when your posts get the most engagement.

**Run a two-week test.** If you don't have data yet:

1. Pick six time slots spread across the day (for example 9:00, 12:00, 15:00, 18:00, 21:00, 23:30 in your audience's main timezone).
2. Post similar content in each slot every day for two weeks.
3. Record impressions, replies and link clicks for each post in a simple sheet.
4. Keep the three or four slots that consistently win.

**Ask.** A poll ("When are you usually on here?") gives you a rough answer and an easy engagement post.

**Mind the timezone.** If most of your followers are in another region, schedule for their evening, not yours.

## Mix the right post types

Every slot should have a job. A healthy mix for most creators:

- **Teasers:** cropped or covered previews that show your style and point to OnlyFans.
- **Personality:** a thought, a joke, a photo from your day, a question. These build connection and replies.
- **Promotion:** a clear offer and your link. "New set out tonight. 7 days free for new subscribers, link in the replies."
- **Engagement:** polls, "this or that", replies to followers.
- **Reposts:** other creators' posts you genuinely like, or partners you trade with.

A good ratio to start with: for every direct promotion, three or four posts that are teasers, personality or engagement.

Adult media on X has to be marked as sensitive. Turn on the sensitive-media setting in X's settings, keep your profile photo and header within X's rules, and read X's adult content policy once before your first scheduled week.

## A sample day

Adjust the times to your own winning slots.

| Time | Post type | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Promotion | Offer plus link, pinned if it does well |
| Late morning | Teaser | Image, short caption, question |
| Midday | Personality or poll | Short, conversational |
| Early evening | Teaser video | Your strongest media of the day |
| Evening | Engagement | Reply to comments, repost partners |
| Late evening | Promotion | Different wording from the morning |

Between posts, spend short blocks replying to comments and engaging with creators in your niche. Engagement is part of the schedule, not extra.

## Batch the work so the schedule survives

A schedule you can't keep is worse than a smaller one you can.

- **One planning session a week:** choose the week's teasers and offers, write captions.
- **One media session:** crop, cover and export everything for the week.
- **Schedule posts** with X's built-in scheduler on the web, or a scheduling tool you trust. Keep live replies human.
- **Keep a swipe file** of your best-performing captions to reuse with new media.

On the OnlyFans side, you can schedule feed posts in OnlyFans itself. If you run Inflowwww, **Auto Post** (`/growth/auto-post`) drips a list of ready-made feed posts out one at a time, and **Auto Story** (`/growth/auto-story`) posts stories from a vault folder. Neither posts to X.

## Review every week

Every Sunday, spend 20 minutes:

1. List your top five posts by engagement and by link clicks. They are often different.
2. Check subscribers from your X-only tracking link in your OnlyFans settings. If you run Inflowwww, the same numbers are in **Tracking links** (`/growth/tracking-links`).
3. Move one weak slot to a new time, or swap its post type.
4. Write next week's captions based on what won.

After a month, your schedule is built from evidence. Keep reviewing; audiences shift with seasons and with your own growth.
