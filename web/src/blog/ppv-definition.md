"No PPV" means a creator sells nothing behind a pay-per-view unlock. The subscription covers everything on the page, and income beyond it comes from the subscription price itself, tips, custom requests and similar choices the fan makes. It suits creators who want simple pricing and a relaxed inbox, and it caps how much any single fan spends unless they choose to tip.

## First, what PPV is

PPV, short for pay-per-view, is content sold on top of the subscription for a one-time price.

- **What can be PPV:** photos, videos, audio, even text. Creators usually reserve it for longer or more exclusive pieces.
- **Where it appears:** as locked posts on the feed, where fans see a preview and pay to unlock, or as locked messages sent in DMs.
- **How it is previewed:** a blurred image, a short clip, or a caption describing what is inside.
- **How it is priced:** the creator sets the price, piece by piece, and can vary it by length, exclusivity or demand.

The platform keeps a share of every sale, as it does with subscriptions and tips. Check OnlyFans' current terms for the exact split.

## What "no PPV" promises the fan

A page that advertises "no PPV" is making a specific promise. Keep all of it, or do not use the label.

- **All-inclusive access.** The subscription unlocks every feed post, past and future.
- **No locked posts and no locked messages.** Nothing on the page asks for a second payment to view general content.
- **Clear pricing.** Fans know exactly what they are paying before they subscribe.
- **Equal access.** Every subscriber sees the same content, whatever else they spend.
- **A quieter inbox.** Messages are conversations, not sales pitches.

## How no-PPV creators still earn more

"No PPV" does not mean "subscription only". Common income on all-inclusive pages:

1. **A higher subscription price,** to reflect that everything is included.
2. **Tips,** including tip goals on posts and thank-you tips after a stream.
3. **Custom requests,** priced individually because they are made for one person.
4. **Live streams,** where fans can tip during the show.
5. **Paid one-to-one time,** such as calls, if that is within your boundaries.
6. **Bundles and discounts** on longer subscriptions, to improve retention.

If you add customs or paid calls, say clearly on your page that these are optional extras, so the "no PPV" promise stays intact.

## Pros and cons

**Pros:**

- easier to attract new subscribers, because the deal is obvious,
- often better retention, since fans do not hit surprise charges,
- steadier, more predictable income,
- no time spent making PPV that few people unlock,
- less pressure to escalate content to justify a separate price.

**Cons:**

- lower ceiling per fan, since your biggest spenders have fewer ways to spend,
- the feed has to stay fresh and valuable every week,
- raising the subscription price can slow growth,
- switching back to PPV later needs careful communication.

## Is no PPV right for you? A checklist

You are a good fit if most of these are true:

- [ ] You can post consistently, several times a week.
- [ ] Your content is valuable enough to justify a higher subscription.
- [ ] Your audience values simplicity and has complained about PPV elsewhere.
- [ ] You would rather spend inbox time talking than selling.
- [ ] A steady monthly income matters more to you than occasional big sales.
