Creators who stream regularly tend to report the same things in Reddit threads: tips arrive faster than they expected, sales pick up after a stream, and fans start treating them as a real person rather than a feed. They report one warning just as often: a stream nobody heard about draws nobody. Here is a plan for your first five streams that takes both seriously.

## What creators keep reporting

Paraphrasing the common threads:

- **Streams make you real.** Fans who have seen you talk, laugh and react live trust you more, and that shows up in messages and renewals.
- **There is a rush afterwards.** Many creators see a wave of DMs and purchases in the hours after a stream.
- **Small audiences can still tip well.** A handful of engaged viewers can outperform a big, passive audience.
- **Promotion is most of the work.** The difference between a busy stream and an empty one is usually the announcement, not the content.
- **Being yourself works.** Creators who chat, play games, cook or draw often say viewers value the interaction more than any performance.

## Before stream one: pick the time with your fans

Do not guess the time slot. Ask.

1. **Post a poll** with three or four time options in your audience's main time zone.
2. **Check when your fans already reply and buy,** and compare.
3. **Pick one slot** and keep it for at least four streams, so fans learn the habit.

A fixed weekly slot ("Thursdays at 8") is easier to remember than a new time every week.

## Promote it like an event

- **Three days out:** feed post with the date, time and what you will be doing.
- **The day before:** a personal message to your regulars and best tippers. Personal invitations convert better than mass announcements.
- **One hour before:** a reminder.
- **On other socials:** a teaser that points people to your page, within each platform's rules.

## Start casual and keep it short

Your first streams do not need a show.

- **15 to 30 minutes** is plenty for a first stream. Leave them wanting more.
- **Open by greeting people by name** as they join. It sets the tone and rewards the early arrivals.
- **Ask easy questions:** how their day went, what they are drinking, what they would like you to do next stream.
- **Do something you enjoy:** a game, a recipe, a drawing, a try-on. Enthusiasm reads well on camera.
- **Close on time,** with a thank-you and the date of the next one.

## Monetise with tips, kindly

On a paid subscription page, subscribers usually join a stream at no extra cost, so tips do the earning. Structure helps:

- **A tiered tip menu** on screen, with clear amounts and rewards inside your boundaries.
- **Shared tip goals** that unlock something for everyone, such as an extra 15 minutes, a song choice or a free post.
- **Games with prizes you decided beforehand,** such as a wheel with a free photo, a shout-out or a discount code.
- **Thank-yous in real time,** and a DM afterwards to anyone who tipped.

Never let a tip goal move a boundary. Decide your menu before you go live and stick to it.

## Handle the hard moments, then review

- **Trolls and rude comments:** do not engage. Remove or block them and move on. If you can, have a friend or team member watching chat to do this for you.
- **Requests outside your menu:** "That's not on the menu tonight" is a complete answer.
- **Tech trouble:** keep a backup connection, and if the stream drops, post a short message so viewers know what happened.
- **Low turnout:** treat it as practice. Talk to whoever is there; they will remember it.
