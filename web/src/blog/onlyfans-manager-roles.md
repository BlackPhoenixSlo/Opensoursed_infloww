An OnlyFans manager runs the business side of a creator's account: messages, promotion, scheduling and reporting, in exchange for a share of earnings or a fixed fee. No qualification is required, so what separates good managers is preparation, how they find creators, and the contract they put in place.

## What the job really is

Most of the work is unglamorous and repeatable:

- Answering fans and selling paid messages, often across shifts.
- Planning the posting calendar and keeping it consistent.
- Promoting the account on social platforms.
- Sending the creator clear numbers every week.
- Handling the admin: team access and shift handovers. The creator keeps their own login and two-factor codes.

Decide which of these you offer as your core package and which are extras. A short, clear menu is easier to sell than "full service".

## Set up before your first client

Have these ready before you pitch anyone:

1. **A focus.** One niche, language or region you understand well. Creators trust specialists.
2. **A one-page site.** Who you work with, what you do, how you report, how to contact you.
3. **A contract template**, reviewed by a lawyer in your jurisdiction (more on that below).
4. **A tool setup.** A shared inbox for your chatters, a place for reply scripts, a scheduler for posts, and reports per creator.
5. **A written process.** How a new chatter is trained, what a shift handover looks like, what is never sent.

## Find creators by being useful first

Cold messages that say "I can grow your account" get ignored. What works better:

- **Show up in creator communities** on Reddit and other forums, and answer questions well. Over a few months, people start asking you.
- **Make a specific offer.** "I noticed your posts are irregular on weekends. I can cover Friday to Sunday chat and posting for a 30-day trial." Specific beats big.
- **Ask for referrals.** A creator who is happy with your weekly report is your best salesperson.
- **Start with established accounts** if you can. Creators who already earn often understand what a manager is worth and can afford one.

Never pressure anyone, and take no for an answer the first time. Your reputation in a small industry is the asset.

## Put the contract in writing

This is not legal advice. Have a lawyer review your template before you use it. The clauses most management contracts need:

- **Term.** Start date, length, and whether it renews.
- **Scope.** Exactly which services are included, and the rate for anything extra.
- **Pay.** A percentage (and of what: gross or net) or a fixed monthly fee, plus the payment date.
- **Late payment.** What happens, and when you may pause work.
- **Confidentiality.** Covering you and everyone you bring in.
- **Exit.** Notice period, handover, and the day your access ends.
- **Content and accounts.** That the creator keeps ownership of their content and their account.
- **Payouts and logins.** Payout details stay in the creator's name and are never changed by the manager; the creator holds the email and two-factor codes.

Walk the creator through it line by line. A contract they understand is one they will honour.

## Your first 30 days with a creator

1. Week 1: agree targets, capture the account session with the creator present, set up chatters.
2. Week 2: write the first scripts and the "never send" list together.
3. Week 3: start segmented mass messages and a fixed posting schedule.
4. Week 4: send a report with three numbers and one plan for next month.

Repeat the report every week after that. Creators renew with managers who make the numbers easy to see.
