Your prices decide two things: who subscribes and who renews. Price too low and you train fans to expect bargains; too high and strangers won't take the chance. The fix is a deliberate structure: a page model, a subscription price you can hold, a ladder of paid extras, and promotions with an end date.

## Free page, paid page, or both

**Free page.** Anyone can follow, so the audience grows faster. Income comes from PPV messages, tips and customs. It suits creators who already have an audience elsewhere and can chat at volume.

**Paid page.** Fewer subscribers, but each one has already paid, and paying fans tend to engage more. Income starts with the subscription and grows with extras.

**Both.** Some creators run a free page for updates and teasers and a paid page for the main content. It widens the funnel, but it is two pages to run. Start with one and add the second when you can keep both active.

A quick way to compare, using your own numbers: estimate monthly followers × share who buy × average spend for the free page, and subscribers × price plus extras for the paid page.

## Setting the subscription price

1. **Research your niche.** Note prices for creators with similar audience size and content.
2. **Pick a price you can hold.** Raising prices later can push existing fans to cancel, so don't start artificially low.
3. **Stay inside the platform's limits.** OnlyFans sets a minimum and maximum subscription price; check the current range in your settings.
4. **Offer bundles.** Multi-month subscriptions at a modest discount reward commitment and improve retention.
5. **Change one thing at a time,** and judge it on total revenue over several weeks.

## Paid extras: the ladder

Most growing accounts earn more from extras than from the subscription.

- **PPV messages:** a few clear price tiers by format and effort.
- **Custom content:** a published minimum, turnaround time and list of what you don't offer.
- **Tip menu:** a short list of small, specific things fans can tip for, pinned to your profile.
- **Personal attention:** regular chat or check-ins as a paid service, with clear terms on what is included.
- **Streams and paid posts:** occasional events that give regulars a reason to show up.

Describe every item honestly. Fans who get what they expected come back.

## Discounts, trials and promotions

Promotions bring in new fans. Run them with a plan:

- **Give every promotion an end date.** Permanent discounts become the real price.
- **Use free trials for new audiences,** such as a launch or a collaboration, and track how many trial fans convert to paid.
- **Win back expired fans** with a returning-subscriber offer, sent once, not every week.
- **Never discount your top extras by default.** Discount the entry point, not the premium.

## Keep subscribers renewing

Renewals are where pricing pays off:

- Post on a schedule fans can predict.
- Reply to messages personally and promptly.
- Thank tippers and buyers by name.
- Check in with fans whose renewal is turned off, without pressure.
- Review prices quarterly, and give regulars notice before any increase.
