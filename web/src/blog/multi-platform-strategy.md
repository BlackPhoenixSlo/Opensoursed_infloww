Expanding to more platforms protects an agency when one platform changes its rules, and it opens new income. Inflowwww has no platform partnerships to announce, so this post skips the press release and gets to the strategy.

## What we support, plainly

Start with the facts, because a strategy built on a tool that cannot do the job fails on day one.

- Inflowwww manages **OnlyFans** accounts. A Fansly client lives in the repo; the Docker image does not ship it yet.
- It does **not** support mym or Fanvue.
- For comparison, Infloww's pricing page has plan cards titled "Infloww for OnlyFans", "Infloww for Fansly", "Infloww for mym" and "Infloww for Fanvue" (infloww.com, captured 2026-10-11).

If your expansion plan depends on mym or Fanvue, you will need another tool for those accounts. If it is OnlyFans plus Fansly, plan for Fansly's own dashboard until the client ships in the image.

## Why expand at all

- **Spread risk.** A rule change, fee change or payout delay on one platform hurts less when it is not the only source of income.
- **Reach new fans.** Fans try new platforms too. Some will never subscribe on the platform your creator started on.
- **Remove a ceiling.** Growth on one platform eventually slows. A second platform with a different audience resets the curve.
- **Learn safely.** A new page is a low-stakes place to test a niche, a price point or a content style.

## Check these before you open an account

**Knowledge.** Every platform has its own rules, payout terms, content policies and features. Someone on your team should read the creator documentation end to end before launch, and follow it.

**Discovery.** Platforms differ in how new fans find creators. OnlyFans relies mostly on traffic from outside the platform. Others put more weight on in-platform search and recommendations. Your promotion plan has to match the platform, not be copied from the last one.

**Audience.** Do not assume existing fans will follow. Plan as if you are building from zero, and treat any fans who move over as a bonus.

**Capacity.** Count the hours. A second page needs content, chat coverage and reporting. If the team is already stretched, hire or wait.

## A 90-day rollout

| Days | Focus | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1-15 | Research and setup | Rules read, profile written, first month of content ready |
| 16-45 | Launch and promote | Posting on a fixed schedule, chat covered, first promotion live |
| 46-75 | Measure and adjust | Weekly numbers compared with the main page, one change per week |
| 76-90 | Decide | Keep, change or close, written up for the next creator |

Use one or two creators for the first rollout, not the whole roster. Agree goals with them in writing, and share the weekly numbers.

## Strategic, not scattered

The agencies that do multi-platform well treat each platform as its own channel with its own plan. A few rules of thumb:

- Give each platform a reason to exist: a different price, content mix or niche.
- Keep the creator's voice consistent, so fans recognise them anywhere.
- Track each platform separately. If one is not earning its hours after 90 days, it is fine to stop.
