In Canada, OnlyFans income is generally business or self-employment income. You report it with your personal return on Form T2125, you can deduct reasonable business expenses, and GST/HST registration becomes required once your revenue passes the small-supplier threshold. The Canada Revenue Agency publishes the current rates, thresholds and dates at [canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html). This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with the CRA or an accountant.

## Reporting the income

Every dollar counts: subscriptions, tips, PPV messages and paid posts.

1. **Treat it as a business.** The CRA generally treats creators who sell their own content as carrying on a business, which is what allows expense deductions.
2. **Fill in Form T2125** (Statement of Business or Professional Activities). It lists your gross income, your expenses and your net profit, and it goes in with your T1 return. The CRA publishes a guide for it; search canada.ca for "T4002".
3. **Use your own records, not just the payout.** Download monthly earnings statements from OnlyFans and reconcile them with your bank deposits. Report income in Canadian dollars; the CRA explains which exchange rates are acceptable.
4. **Pay CPP contributions on self-employment profit.** These are calculated on your return. The current rates are on canada.ca.

In Québec, you also file a provincial return with Revenu Québec, contribute to the QPP instead of the CPP, and QST registration rules apply alongside GST.

## Setting money aside

Nothing is withheld, so the bill arrives all at once unless you plan for it.

- Open a separate account for tax and move a fixed share of every payout into it.
- Choose that share with an accountant, or by running last year's numbers. Federal and provincial rates both apply and both are progressive. The CRA publishes the current brackets each year, and each province publishes its own.
- If your net tax owing is over the CRA's instalment threshold this year and in either of the two previous years, you may have to pay instalments; the CRA's instalment reminders tell you what and when.

## Deductible expenses

You can generally deduct costs incurred to earn business income, if they are reasonable and you keep receipts.

- **Equipment:** cameras, lighting, computers. Larger items are usually claimed over several years as capital cost allowance (CCA) rather than all at once.
- **Software and services:** editing tools, cloud storage, hosting.
- **Promotion:** ads and paid placements.
- **Wardrobe and props:** only items used for the business and not for daily life. Be conservative here.
- **Home office:** a share of rent, utilities and internet, if you meet the CRA's work-space conditions. Read those conditions before you claim.

The CRA lists common business expenses on canada.ca. When an item is borderline, ask a professional.

## GST/HST registration

GST/HST is a sales tax, not an income tax.

- **The small-supplier rule:** once your taxable revenue passes the small-supplier threshold over four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register. Check the current threshold on canada.ca.
- **Watch it monthly.** If you are close, plan the registration before you cross it.
- **Ask how OnlyFans handles it.** Platforms may collect GST/HST on some sales themselves. Read OnlyFans' current tax help pages and confirm with the CRA or an accountant what you still need to charge and file.
- **Input tax credits:** once registered, you can generally recover the GST/HST you paid on business purchases.

## Deadlines and catching up

- Self-employed individuals have a later filing deadline than employees, but any balance owing is due earlier, and interest starts from that earlier date. The CRA publishes both dates each year.
- File on time even if you can't pay in full. Late-filing penalties are separate from interest.
- If you missed previous years, look up the CRA's **Voluntary Disclosures Program** on canada.ca. Coming forward first is usually better than waiting to be asked.
