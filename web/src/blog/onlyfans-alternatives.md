There are many places to sell subscriptions and content besides OnlyFans, and lists of "the top alternatives" go out of date fast because fees, rules and features change. This post gives you a way to evaluate any platform yourself, then sorts the common options by type.

## First, decide why you are looking

Your reason shapes which alternative makes sense:

- **More reach.** You want a platform with stronger in-platform discovery.
- **Different pricing.** You want subscription tiers, a store, or a different mix of subscriptions and paid content.
- **Less risk.** You want a second income stream so one platform is not everything.
- **Different content.** Your work fits a general creator platform better than an adult-focused one, or the other way round.

Write your reason down. It is the first line of the scorecard below.

## The scorecard: eight questions for any platform

Check each answer on the platform's own official pages, and note the date you checked.

1. **Content rules.** Is your content allowed? Read the terms of service and acceptable use policy in full, not a summary.
2. **Fees.** What percentage does the platform keep, and does it differ by product type (subscriptions, tips, messages, store sales)?
3. **Payouts.** How long until earnings can be withdrawn? Is there a minimum? Which payout methods work in your country?
4. **Discovery.** Can new fans find you on the platform, or does all traffic have to come from outside?
5. **Features.** Subscription tiers, paid messages, live streams, a store, custom requests: which ones does your plan need?
6. **Verification.** What does the platform require from you and from anyone who appears in your content?
7. **Support.** How do creators reach support, and what do creators in communities say about response times?
8. **Track record.** How long has it operated, and how has it handled policy changes in the past?

Score each one, then compare platforms on the questions that matter most for your reason.

## The main types of platforms

**Adult-focused subscription platforms.** Fansly is the most common pairing with OnlyFans, and is known for offering multiple subscription tiers. Others creators often mention include LoyalFans, FanCentro, Fanvue and MYM. Features, fees and discovery vary, so run the scorecard on each.

**General creator platforms.** Patreon and similar services serve a broad range of creators and have stricter rules on adult content than adult-focused platforms. They can suit creators whose work is mostly safe for work, or who want a separate, mainstream-friendly income stream. Read their community guidelines carefully.

**Clip stores and marketplaces.** Platforms such as ManyVids focus on selling individual videos and products rather than only subscriptions. They can complement a subscription page well.

**Your own site.** Some services let you run a paid site on your own domain. You keep more control, but you take on more of the work: payment processing, compliance and bringing every visitor yourself.

## Test before you commit

Do not move your whole business at once.

- Open the new account and complete verification before you announce anything.
- Prepare a month of content for it in advance.
- Promote it to a small part of your audience first, and keep your existing page running as normal.
- After 60 days, compare earnings per hour of work. Keep the new platform if it earns its time.
