No single setting stops content theft. What works is layers: knowing your rights, securing your account, marking your content so it stays traceable, removing what leaks, and limiting who on your team can touch what. Each layer makes theft harder and takedowns easier. This post covers them in order.

## Know your rights

In most countries, you own the copyright in original photos, videos and writing you create from the moment you create them. That means others cannot copy, repost or sell them without your permission.

- **Keep your originals.** Store the full-resolution files with their original dates. They are your evidence of authorship.
- **Note what you published where and when.** A simple spreadsheet of post dates helps if you ever need to prove you were first.
- **Understand takedown notices.** Under laws such as the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act, copyright owners can ask websites and hosts to remove infringing copies. Many countries have equivalents.

This is general information, not legal advice. For serious or repeated theft, talk to a lawyer who works with creators.

## Use the platform's reporting process

OnlyFans has a process for reporting content stolen from creators, and it can pursue removals on your behalf. Look in the official help centre for the stolen content report, and use it whenever you find your work elsewhere.

When you report:

1. **Collect the links** to every copy you find.
2. **Take screenshots** that show the page and the date.
3. **Identify the original** on your page so the match is clear.
4. **Keep a log** of what you reported and when, so you can follow up.

There are also paid services that search for leaked copies and file takedowns for you. If you use one, check its reviews, its pricing and what access it asks for before signing up.

## Lock down your account

A surprising amount of "leaked" content leaks from compromised accounts.

- **Use a unique, strong password** for OnlyFans and for the email account linked to it. A password manager makes this easy.
- **Turn on two-factor authentication,** preferably with an authenticator app rather than SMS where you can.
- **Review Privacy & Safety settings.** Look at what is visible on your profile, and turn on any video protection options the platform offers. Treat these as one layer, not a guarantee.
- **Check active sessions** regularly and sign out anything you do not recognise.
- **Never share your password** with chatters, editors or agencies. Use a tool that gives each person their own login, never the creator's password. OnlyFans' [terms](https://onlyfans.com/terms) allow creators to use assistants, but the account holder stays responsible for everything sent, and fans must not be deceived about who they are talking to in a way that breaks the platform's rules or the law.

## Mark your content

Watermarks do not stop someone from copying content, but they make stolen copies traceable and less valuable.

- **Add a visible watermark** with your page name to photos and videos, placed where it is hard to crop out without ruining the image.
- **Vary placement** across a set so one crop does not remove all of them.
- **Consider per-fan marks** for high-value custom content, such as a small detail or code unique to that buyer, so you can tell who shared it.
- **Keep watermarks tasteful,** so they protect the work without spoiling it.

## Limit who can touch your files

If you work with a team, every extra person with full access is an extra risk.

- **Give each person their own login,** with access only to what their job needs.
- **Remove access on the day someone leaves.**
- **Keep the vault organised** so you know what exists and who sends it.
- **Store backups somewhere you control,** with encryption if you can.

## Make fans want to protect you

The softest layer is the relationship. Fans who feel a real connection are less likely to share and more likely to tell you when they see your content elsewhere. Thank fans who report leaks, and say clearly on your page that sharing content is not allowed.

If a leak is causing you distress, take a break, talk to someone you trust and look for creator support communities. Protecting your work matters, and so do you.
