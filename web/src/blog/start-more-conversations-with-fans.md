Fans on OnlyFans tend to have short visits and crowded inboxes. A message that arrives at the wrong time, says the same thing as yesterday's, or interrupts a live chat gets ignored or, worse, gets someone to unsubscribe. Starting more conversations is mostly about sending fewer, better-timed, better-targeted messages. Here is how, and what Inflowwww does for each step today.

## 1. Send when fans are actually around

Timing beats volume.

- **Find your peak hours.** Look at when your fans reply and buy, not when it suits you. Most audiences have one or two clear windows a day, and they shift with time zones.
- **Schedule into those windows.** Write messages when you have time, send them when fans are online.
- **Stagger big sends.** If you message a large list, splitting it across an hour feels less like a blast.

If you run Inflowwww, **Smart Messages** (`/growth/smart-messages`) has a Mass tab and a Scheduled tab with a composer, so you can queue the week's messages in one sitting. The default install does not ship an auto-messenger that fires when a fan comes online; you choose when things go out.

## 2. Segment before you send

A message written for everyone is written for nobody.

Useful segments for most pages:

- **New subscribers** from the last week: welcome and an easy question.
- **Regular buyers:** first look at new content.
- **Quiet fans** who have not replied in a few weeks: a low-pressure check-in, no offer.
- **Expiring or lapsed subscribers:** a reason to stay.

Build the segments in **Smart lists** (`/growth/smart-lists`) from rules on spend, recency, subscription status and tags, then use them as the audience of a mass message. Even a small change, such as mentioning that the fan is new, lifts reply rates compared with a generic blast.

## 3. Do not interrupt a live conversation

Few things feel more automated than a mass message landing in the middle of a real chat.

Before any mass send:

1. **Check who your team is talking to right now,** and leave them out, or schedule the send for after the shift.
2. **Skip fans who just got similar content,** so nobody receives the same teaser twice.
3. **Reply to fans you owe a reply** before sending them anything new. A broadcast to someone you left on read is worse than silence.

A saved smart list can also be set as an exclusion in the mass composer, for example fans who bought recently. The rest is a team habit: look at the chat list in **Messages Pro** (`/messages-pro`) before you send, and agree on send times with your chatters.

## 4. Hand off to a human quickly

A mass message only starts the conversation. The reply is what matters.

- **Watch for replies after every send,** and answer the first wave within minutes if you can.
- **Write the first follow-up personally.** Reference what the fan said, not what the broadcast said.
- **Move toward an offer only when it fits.** If the fan wants to chat, chat.

Replies land in the chat list in Messages Pro, with the thread and fan insights side by side. Common follow-ups can be kept in **Scripts** (`/growth/scripts`), with a blank for something personal.

## 5. Say thank you after a purchase

A short thank-you after a fan buys is one of the most reliable ways to get a second conversation.

- **Send it the same day.**
- **Keep it personal:** one line about what they bought or something they said.
- **Add something small and free** now and then, such as a photo or a voice note. A gift without an ask builds goodwill that an offer cannot.

The **Message dashboard** (`/analytics/message-dashboard`) logs PPVs and tips, so you can see who bought today and make sure nobody goes unthanked.

## A weekly checklist

- Peak hours confirmed for each creator.
- Two or three segmented mass messages scheduled.
- Send times agreed with the team, away from live shifts.
- Replies to every send answered within the hour.
- Thank-yous sent for every purchase.

None of this needs a subscription to any tool. It needs a calendar, a list and some restraint.
