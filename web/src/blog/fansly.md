New to marketing a creator on Fansly? Start with four habits: a profile built for the feed, a posting rhythm you can keep, tracked outside traffic, and a weekly look at the numbers. This page is the short version, with links to the longer guides.

## The four habits that matter most

**1. Build the profile for discovery.** Fansly has a For You feed, so free, follower-visible posts can reach people who have never heard of the creator. Write a bio that states what the page offers and how often it posts. Use tags that describe the content honestly.

**2. Post on a rhythm, not in bursts.** Three to five feed posts a week, every week, is a better start than twenty in one week and none the next. Batch content so a busy week does not break the schedule.

**3. Send outside traffic, and track it.** Link the Fansly profile from the creator's other channels, with one tracked link per source. Within a few weeks you will know which sources bring subscribers and which only bring clicks.

**4. Read the numbers weekly.** Follower growth, renewals, paid message sales, message performance and free-to-paid conversion. Change one thing per week and write down what you changed.

## Running Fansly next to OnlyFans

Many agencies add Fansly for creators who already do well on OnlyFans. A few rules make that easier.

- **Do not mirror everything.** Fansly's feed rewards free, follower-visible posts more than OnlyFans does. Plan the feed separately.
- **Price per platform.** Fans compare. Decide whether the two pages offer different things or the same thing at the same price, and say so clearly.
- **Keep one fan record per platform.** Fans on Fansly and OnlyFans are separate accounts, so keep separate notes, but check both before you make an offer so nobody is sold the same set twice.
- **Staff by volume.** Check how many chats each platform produces per day before you split chatter hours.

## Chat habits that hold up on any platform

- Reply to new subscribers quickly. The first conversation sets the tone for the subscription.
- Keep a library of tested openers. Rewrite them in the creator's voice; never paste the same line to everyone.
- Make offers small and relevant to the conversation. Large, blunt offers end threads.
- Respect the fan's pace. A fan who says no today may buy next week if the conversation stays pleasant.
