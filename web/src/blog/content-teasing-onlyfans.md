A teaser is a promise with a date on it. Done well, it tells a fan what they will get, why it is worth opening, and when. Here are seven ways to build that anticipation, and a simple weekly rhythm to run them.

## Write descriptions that sell the moment

A caption should answer one question: why open this now?

- **Lead with the specific.** "New set from Saturday's rooftop shoot, 24 photos" beats "new pics".
- **Name the value.** Count of photos, length of the clip, anything that is only in this set.
- **Give a deadline when there is one.** "Price goes up Sunday" works only if it is true.
- **End with a question or a call to action.** "Which look should I post next?" invites a reply.

Write three versions of every important caption and pick the shortest one that still says all of the above.

## Preview images and sneak-peek clips

The preview is the shop window.

- **Crop, do not hide.** A tight crop of the real set is more honest and more persuasive than a heavy blur.
- **Pick the second-best frame.** Keep the best one inside the PPV so the unlock feels like an upgrade.
- **Clip length:** 5-15 seconds for a video teaser. Cut before the moment, not after it.
- **Same light, same outfit.** The preview must match what the fan unlocks. Mismatched previews create refund requests and erode trust.

## Behind-the-scenes and exclusive insight

Not every tease needs to be a crop of the content. Share the making-of: the outfit you chose, the location scouting, the lighting test. Then say when the finished set lands. This builds anticipation without giving the set away, and it is content in its own right.

## Temporary teasers

Stories and short-lived posts create urgency on their own because they disappear.

- Post a story preview 24 hours before a drop.
- Post a second one, with a different frame, an hour before.
- Delete or let both expire after the drop.

## Questions and storytelling

**Ask before you reveal.** "Red or black for Friday's set?" A poll makes the fan a co-author, and co-authors open the result.

**Tell it in parts.** Turn a themed shoot into a three-part arc across a week: setting the scene on Monday, the middle on Wednesday, the finale as PPV on Friday. Each part ends by naming the next date.

A reveal is only satisfying if there is something to reveal.

## A weekly teasing rhythm

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Monday | Behind-the-scenes post about the week's set |
| Tuesday | Poll: one choice about the set |
| Wednesday | Cropped preview image on the feed |
| Thursday | Story teaser, auto-expiring |
| Friday | PPV drop by message, with a clear caption |
| Saturday | Thank the buyers; tease next week |

### Do and don't

- **Do** keep every promise you tease. If the set is 20 photos, say 20.
- **Do** space teasers out. One tease per day is enough.
- **Don't** tease something you have not shot yet with a firm date.
- **Don't** send the same teaser to fans who already bought the set. Use a list that excludes buyers.
