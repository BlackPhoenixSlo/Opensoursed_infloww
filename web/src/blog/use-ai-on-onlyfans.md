AI tools can save creators real time on editing, captions and planning. They also come with platform rules that you need to follow exactly. Start with the rules, then use AI for the jobs it does well, and keep a human in charge of anything a fan reads as you.

## Start with the platform's rules

OnlyFans has published rules on AI-generated and AI-altered content. As commonly summarised, and as described in many creator guides, they include:

- **AI content must feature the verified creator.** You cannot post AI-generated people who are not you.
- **AI content must be labelled.** Creators are expected to tag AI-generated or AI-enhanced posts, commonly with "#ai".
- **Fans should not be misled about who they are messaging.** Using AI chatbots to talk to fans as the creator is restricted.

Rules change, and summaries go out of date. Read the current OnlyFans Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy yourself before you use any AI tool on your page, and check again every few months. If anything is unclear, ask OnlyFans support. When in doubt, label it and keep a human in the conversation.

## Use AI to polish visuals

Within those rules, AI editing tools are useful for the boring parts of production:

- **Clean-up:** removing a stray object from the background, fixing lighting or white balance, sharpening a slightly soft shot.
- **Consistency:** applying the same look across a set so it feels like one shoot.
- **Formatting:** cropping and resizing for feed posts, previews and other platforms.
- **Video basics:** trimming, stabilising, cleaning up audio.

Try a few tools on the same photo before you commit, and read the privacy terms: you are uploading private content to someone else's server. Prefer tools that let you delete uploads, and avoid any tool that claims rights to what you upload. Label anything substantially altered, as the rules require.

## Use AI to help you write, then rewrite it yourself

AI is a decent first-draft machine for:

- caption ideas and post titles,
- your bio, especially if you are stuck on the first line,
- a list of username ideas when you are starting out,
- a weekly content calendar outline.

Then edit. Fans come for a real person, so the final version should sound like you: your phrases, your humour, your spelling habits. A good rule is that nothing AI-written goes out without you changing at least a few words of it.

For messages to fans, keep it human. Saved replies written by you or your team, kept in **Scripts** (`/growth/scripts`) if you run Inflowwww, give you speed without handing the conversation to a model. Inflowwww has no AI Copilot, and its default build has no AI writing messages to fans.

## Use data, with or without AI, to see what works

You do not need an AI tool to learn what your audience likes. You need data and a regular habit of looking at it:

- Which posts get the most likes, comments and tips?
- Which PPV formats get opened, at what prices?
- When do fans reply and buy?

Spreadsheets work. So do **Creator reports** (`/analytics/creator-reports`), **Fan reports** (`/analytics/fan-reports`) and the **Message dashboard** (`/analytics/message-dashboard`) if you run Inflowwww. If you then want an AI assistant to help summarise the trends, give it numbers, not private conversations.

## Use AI for ideas, not identity

AI is good at breaking a creative block. Ask it for ten themes for next month, five ways to reuse a location, or seasonal ideas for a holiday. Pick the ones that fit you and ignore the rest.

What AI should not do is decide who you are. Your persona, your boundaries and your relationship with fans are the parts of the page nobody else can replicate. Protect them.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] I have read the current OnlyFans rules on AI myself.
- [ ] Every AI-generated or AI-altered post features only me and is labelled.
- [ ] I know where each AI tool stores my uploads, and I can delete them.
- [ ] Captions and bios are edited into my own voice.
- [ ] A human, me or my team, writes every message fans receive.
