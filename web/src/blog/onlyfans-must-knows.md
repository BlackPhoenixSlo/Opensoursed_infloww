OnlyFans can be a real business, but it is more work than it looks from outside, and some choices are hard to undo. Here are five things worth weighing before you sign up, written plainly.

## 1. It is a job, with job-sized hours

Running a page means shooting, editing, posting, promoting on other platforms, answering messages and keeping records. Many creators spend more time on promotion and messaging than on making content.

- Estimate your weekly hours honestly before you start: content, promotion, messages, admin.
- Decide whether this is a side income or a main income, and plan your time to match.
- Earnings are taxable in most countries. This is not tax advice. Check the current rules with your country's tax authority or an accountant.

## 2. You will learn photography and video basics

Good lighting, framing and simple editing make a visible difference. You do not need expensive gear, but you will need to learn.

- Start with light: a window or a ring light does more than a new camera.
- Learn basic composition, such as keeping the subject centred and the background tidy.
- Pick one editing app and one consistent look.

## 3. Staying anonymous is hard

This deserves serious thought. Content can be saved and shared beyond the platform, and people can be recognised from small details. Being identified without your consent can affect family, work and personal safety.

- Decide before you start whether you will show your face, and accept that you may be recognised either way.
- Use a stage name, a separate email and a separate phone number.
- Keep identifying details out of frame: recognisable views from windows, mail, uniforms, distinctive jewellery or tattoos.
- Remove location metadata from files before uploading.
- Think through who in your life you would tell, and how, if it came out.

**Turn off contact syncing.** Social apps that sync your phone contacts can suggest your promotion accounts to people you know. Turn off contact syncing and "suggest my account to others" settings on every app you use for promotion, and keep promotion accounts on a separate phone number where possible.

## 4. You need to be active, and you need to read the rules

A page that posts rarely and replies slowly loses subscribers. Fans renew because there is something new and because they feel heard.

- Set a posting schedule you can keep for months.
- Answer messages in fixed windows each day.
- Read the [Terms of Service](https://onlyfans.com/terms) and the Acceptable Use Policy before your first upload. They cover what is allowed, who may appear in content, how co-creators are verified and how payouts work.

## 5. Build your library before launch

A page with a few dozen posts on day one converts far better than an empty one. Shoot in batches before you go live, keep a backlog for slow weeks, and add variety in setting, outfit and format so the feed does not repeat itself.

## Weighing it up

OnlyFans gives creators control over their content, prices and schedule, and some build a stable income from it. It also asks for real hours, real skills and real thought about privacy. If you have weighed all of these points and still want to start, you are starting better prepared than most.
