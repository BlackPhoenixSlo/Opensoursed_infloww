OnlyFans is a subscription platform: fans pay creators for access to their content, and creators earn from subscriptions, paid messages, tips and paid posts. Doing well on it takes a plan, a finished profile, real privacy habits and steady promotion. This guide covers each, in order.

## What OnlyFans is and how creators earn

Creators post to a feed that subscribers can see, and message fans directly. Income comes from several places:

- **Subscriptions:** a monthly price you set, or a free page.
- **Paid messages (PPV):** content sent in a direct message with a price to unlock it.
- **Tips:** voluntary payments on posts, messages or streams.
- **Paid posts and customs:** individual items or requests at a price you agree.

OnlyFans keeps 20% of each payment before VAT, per its terms at the time of writing; check the current figure in its Help Center. It pays out the rest to your bank or payout account.

## Make a plan before you sign up

Decide these on paper first. They are much harder to change after launch.

- **Niche and persona:** what your page is about and how you come across.
- **Boundaries:** what you will and will not create, and whether you show your face.
- **Stage name and username:** short, easy to spell, available on every platform you will use.
- **Pricing model:** free, paid, or both.
- **Schedule:** how many posts a week, and when you answer messages.
- **Budget:** equipment, lighting and any promotion spend.

## Set up the profile

A complete profile converts visitors. Add a sharp profile picture, a banner that matches it, and a bio that says what you post, how often and what the visitor should do next. Write a short welcome message so every new subscriber hears from you right away.

## Protect your privacy

This part deserves real time.

- Use a separate email address and phone number for creator work.
- Remove location metadata from photos and videos before uploading.
- Keep identifying details out of frame: windows with recognisable views, mail, uniforms, distinctive tattoos if you want to stay private.
- Watermark your content with your username.
- Turn off contact syncing on social apps you use for promotion, so they do not suggest your accounts to people you know.
- Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere.

## Read the Terms and the Privacy Policy

They are long, but they decide what is allowed, how payouts work, how co-creators must be verified and what happens if there is a dispute. Read them once in full, note the parts that affect you, and check for updates every few months.

## Build a library, then promote

Upload a few dozen posts before you start promoting, so new visitors see a page worth subscribing to. Then promote steadily on two or three other platforms, after reading each one's adult-content policy. Use a consistent handle and link everywhere.

Expect slow growth in the first months. Track a few numbers monthly, change one thing at a time, and keep a schedule you can sustain.
