A good agency takes chatting, promotion and scheduling off your plate in exchange for a share of what you earn. A bad one is easy to spot: it will not put its promises in writing. Here is how to tell them apart before you sign.

## What a good agency actually does

Agencies vary, but the useful ones cover some mix of these:

- Chatting with fans, often in shifts so replies keep coming at night.
- Promotion on social platforms and through collaborations.
- Content planning: what to shoot, when to post, what to price.
- Reporting: weekly numbers you can read without a call.

Decide which of these you need before you talk to anyone. If you only need help with messages, a full-service package means paying for work you did not ask for. We are a CRM, not an agency, so our part of this post is just the checklist.

## Check 1: Results with creators like you

Size is not the signal. Ask for evidence that the agency has grown accounts similar to yours in niche, audience and starting size. Good questions:

- "Show me a before and after for a creator who started where I am, with their permission."
- "How long did it take, and what did you change?"
- "How many creators does each chatter or manager handle?"

Vague answers ("we 10x everyone") are a reason to keep looking.

## Check 2: Terms in writing, before the first login

Read the whole contract, and get anything unclear explained in writing. The clauses that matter most:

| Clause | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Split | The exact percentage, and whether it is taken from gross or net earnings |
| Payment | Who receives the payout, and on what date the split is settled |
| Term | How long you are committed, and whether it renews automatically |
| Exit | Notice period, and what happens to access on the last day |
| Content | Who owns what you create, during and after the contract |
| Confidentiality | That it covers everyone on their team who sees your account |

Have a lawyer you choose look at it before you sign. This is not legal advice, and a short review costs far less than a bad contract.

## Check 3: How they communicate and report

You should never have to chase your own numbers. Ask:

- Who is my named contact, and how fast do they reply?
- What do I get every week, and on which day?
- How do I tell the team something is off limits, and how do you make sure every chatter sees it?

A sample weekly report is a fair thing to request on the first call. If they cannot show one, they probably do not send one.

## Check 4: References you can reach

Testimonials on a website are a start. Talking to people is better. Ask for two current creators and one who has left, and message them yourself. Ask the former client how the exit went: an agency's character shows most at the end.

Creator communities are also a good place to ask quietly, for example r/CreatorsAdvice and r/OnlyFansAdvice on Reddit.

## Check 5: You keep control of the account

The account is yours. Whatever the arrangement, keep these with you:

- The login, the email address and the payout details.
- The final say on what is posted and what is never sent.
- A way to remove the agency's access the same day you decide to.

An agency that asks for your bank login, or for your password over a DM, has told you how it handles risk.

**Or do it yourself.** Plenty of creators run their own accounts, sometimes with one assistant. The trade is more of your time for a bigger share of your earnings. If you go that way, the work is the same as an agency's: answer messages, plan posts, track what sells. Tools help, and a community of other creators helps more.
