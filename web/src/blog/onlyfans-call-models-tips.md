The first video call with a prospective creator is where an agency earns or loses trust. Creators are often talking to several agencies at once, so the one that arrives prepared, shows real numbers and states its terms plainly usually wins.

## Before the call: prepare what they will see

- **Set the scene.** A quiet room, a plain background, a working camera and microphone. Test the link ten minutes early.
- **Research the creator.** Look at their public socials and page. Note one or two specific things you would change and one thing they already do well.
- **Build a short deck.** Five to eight slides, open in a separate tab so you can switch to it smoothly.

What the deck should cover:

1. **Who you are.** How the agency started, who is on the team and what each person does.
2. **Results, with permission.** Before-and-after numbers from creators who agreed to be shown, with names hidden if they prefer. If you are new, say so, and show what you did for your first client, even if it was a friend you worked with for free.
3. **How you work.** Promotion channels, content planning, how fan messaging is handled and who handles it.
4. **What makes you different.** A specific niche, a tested process, a better reporting rhythm. Something checkable, not "we are the best".

Never inflate numbers. Creators talk to each other, and a made-up figure costs you more than a modest real one.

## During the call: listen first

- **Spend a few minutes on small talk.** You may work together for a year; start like it.
- **Ask before you pitch.** What are their goals? How many hours a week do they want to spend? What has frustrated them about the page so far? Have they worked with an agency before, and why did it end?
- **Introduce the agency briefly,** then go to the slides that answer what they just told you.
- **Talk about boundaries early.** Ask what they will and will not do, and make clear the agency follows those limits in every conversation with fans. This is often the question that decides the call.
- **Invite questions** and answer them directly. "I don't know, I'll find out and email you today" is a fine answer.

## Talking terms honestly

Once the creator is interested, move to terms. Be clear and put everything in writing.

- **Contract length.** Many agencies ask for 12 months. Offer a shorter option, such as 6 months or a trial period, for creators who want to see results first.
- **Fee structure.** Offer two options so the creator can choose. For example, a setup fee with a lower revenue share, or no setup fee with a higher share.
- **What the fee pays for.** List the services: chatting hours, promotion, content planning, reporting. Ambiguity here causes most disputes later.
- **Exit terms.** How either side can end the agreement, with how much notice, and what happens to account access afterwards.
- **Account and content ownership.** The creator owns their page and their content. Say it in the contract.

This is not legal advice; have a lawyer in your jurisdiction draft or review these clauses.

Encourage the creator to read the contract in their own time and, if they want, to have someone independent review it. An agency that is confident in its terms does not need a signature in the next five minutes.

Showing the creator how they will see their numbers helps. If you run Inflowwww, **Creator reports** (`/analytics/creator-reports`) cover revenue per creator, so you can tell them what they will be looking at each month. It costs the same whether they earn $400 or $40,000: $0 at every bracket.

## Closing and next steps

If you agree on terms, send the contract during or right after the call and offer to stay on to answer questions. If they want time, agree a follow-up date before you hang up.

End with a concrete onboarding plan:

1. The date of the first strategy session.
2. What you need from them: content schedule, persona notes, boundaries list.
3. How account access will be set up, and who on your team will have it.
4. When they will receive the first report.

Then follow up in writing the same day with everything you agreed.
